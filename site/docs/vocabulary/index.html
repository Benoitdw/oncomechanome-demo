This page is generated from oncomechanome/chain/vocabulary.py, the single source of truth. The Pydantic models, the providers’ JSON Schemas and the text of the prompts are all derived from that same module, so a label added there appears everywhere — including here.

How a label is used

A step carries one label plus exactly one value for each slot that label declares. Slot values are written "<slot>: <value>" as a flat list of strings rather than a nested mapping, for a reason worth knowing: the natural encoding — one object type per label, each with its own slot enum — needs oneOf with a discriminator, which structured-output modes reject. The flat form is the largest common denominator, and it lets a label declare two slots without a second field (Altered binding specificity takes from: and to:).

Detail belongs in a step’s free-text note and in target, never in the vocabulary. target names the one partner or residue the step concerns — TIPRL1, H3K36me2 — and is what distinguishes two steps carrying the same label and the same slot value.

Every label describes the mutant protein itself. An effect on another protein downstream — a substrate no longer phosphorylated, a pathway whose output drops — is not a step.

One step per partner

A step is one event on one partner. A variant that strengthens binding to three proteins carries three Increase binding affinity steps, each with its own target, evidence and quantity; BRAF V600E, active without activation-loop phosphorylation and without Ras recruitment, carries two Loss of regulatory dependence steps. The validator rejects a slot carrying two values, because a step bundling several partners cannot say how much of the effect falls on any one of them.

A Loss label — Loss of binding affinity, Loss of an activity — is used only when the source reports the interaction or the activity absent, or below its assay’s detection limit. Anything weaker is a Decrease.

A self-contact of defined stoichiometry — a dimer, a tetramer — is a binding label with: self. The higher-order assembly labels are for assemblies of indefinite stoichiometry: condensates, fibrils, aggregates, filaments.

Quantities

A label says what changes; a step may also say by how much. The quantity is optional — most sources give a direction without a figure — and when present it carries its own source and a verbatim quote of the figure:

FieldMeaning
measureWhat was measured: Kd, kcat/Km, half-life, ΔΔG of unfolding…
comparator=, ~, <, > — = reported, ~ approximate, < / > a bound such as a detection limit
value, unitThe mutant’s value as reported, or the reported difference for a ΔΔG or a ΔTm
fold_changeMutant ÷ wild-type of measure as the source reports it, never inverted
rawThe figure as the source phrases it, e.g. ~10-fold tighter
source, quoteWhere the figure comes from, and the sentence that carries it

fold_change is not oriented along the label: a Kd ten times higher is 10 on a Decrease binding affinity step. The label gives the direction and the number stays the source’s, so no model is ever asked to invert a figure. It is left null for a difference (ΔΔG, ΔTm) and where the wild type has no value to divide by.

Each label below lists what its quantity usually measures. Two take none: Interactome rewiring, whose figures live on the binding steps it follows from, and Loss of PTM site, which removes the residue outright.

Glyphs

Each label carries a hand-drawn schematic, shown on every step card and listed under “Label glyphs” on the Genes page. A per-variant drawing cannot be produced automatically in a way worth trusting — free-hand SVG from a model is unverifiable, and a structural render shows where the residue sits without saying what the mechanism is. What makes it tractable is that this vocabulary is closed: the picture belongs to the label, is drawn once, and the annotation composes the chain for free. target supplies the caption, so one glyph serves every variant acting on a different partner.

They are deliberately abstract. A glyph illustrates a category; it must not be read as a depiction of one variant’s structure.

They are plain SVG files under site/src/assets/glyphs/, named after the label, edited by hand. A label added to the vocabulary without a matching file falls back to a neutral shape and shows as a gap in the legend rather than breaking anything.

Coverage

covered: false with an uncovered_reason is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of this vocabulary.

### Interactions

- **Increase binding affinity** — Strengthens an interaction the wild-type protein already makes.
  - `with:` `protein`, `self`, `ligand`, `nucleotide`, `substrate`, `DNA`, `RNA`, `membrane`, `metal ion`
  - quantity, when reported: Kd; Ki; kon/koff; competition IC50; relative BRET or pulldown signal
- **Decrease binding affinity** — Weakens an interaction the wild-type protein makes, without abolishing it.
  - `with:` `protein`, `self`, `ligand`, `nucleotide`, `substrate`, `DNA`, `RNA`, `membrane`, `metal ion`
  - quantity, when reported: Kd; Ki; kon/koff; competition IC50; relative BRET or pulldown signal
- **Loss of binding affinity** — Abolishes an existing contact: the source reports the interaction absent or below the detection limit of its assay. A weaker interaction is a decrease, not a loss.
  - `with:` `protein`, `self`, `ligand`, `nucleotide`, `substrate`, `DNA`, `RNA`, `membrane`, `metal ion`
  - quantity, when reported: detection limit of the binding assay
- **Altered binding specificity** — The site still binds, but what it accepts changes. Both ends are recorded: what is no longer recognised, and what is now.
  - `from:` `cognate ligand`, `cognate substrate`, `cognate protein`, `cognate DNA`, `cognate RNA`
  - `to:` `non-cognate ligand`, `antagonist`, `non-cognate substrate`, `non-cognate protein`, `non-cognate DNA`, `non-cognate RNA`
  - quantity, when reported: preference ratio between the new and the former partner
- **New binding interface** — Creates an interface for a partner the wild-type protein does not bind at all.
  - `with:` `protein`, `self`, `ligand`, `DNA`, `RNA`, `membrane`
  - quantity, when reported: Kd of the new complex
- **Interactome rewiring** — The set of partners changes as a whole. A consequence of several binding steps, which carry the quantities; this step carries none.
  - `toward:` `gained partners`, `lost partners`, `shifted target set`
  - quantity: none — leave `quantity` null

### Conformation and regulation

- **Loss of autoinhibition** — Frees a functional module from a restraint carried by the protein itself or by a bound regulator.
  - `of:` `intramolecular contact`, `autoinhibitory segment`, `regulatory subunit`, `pseudosubstrate`
  - quantity, when reported: conformational biosensor signal (FRET)
- **Gain of autoinhibition** — Locks a functional module under a restraint the wild-type protein does not impose, or tightens one it does.
  - `of:` `intramolecular contact`, `autoinhibitory segment`, `regulatory subunit`, `pseudosubstrate`
  - quantity, when reported: conformational biosensor signal (FRET)
- **Conformational equilibrium shift** — Shifts the population of conformers toward another state than the wild-type protein favours.
  - `toward:` `active state`, `inactive state`, `open state`, `closed state`, `high-affinity state`, `low-affinity state`
  - quantity, when reported: equilibrium constant; fraction in the active state; ΔG between states
- **Loss of regulatory dependence** — The protein performs its activity without the input that normally gates it: it is active when the input is absent.
  - `independent of:` `ligand`, `upstream kinase`, `upstream GTPase`, `cofactor`, `nucleotide`, `voltage`, `GEF/GAP`, `membrane recruitment`, `allosteric inhibitor`, `allosteric activator`, `proteolytic activation`
  - quantity, when reported: activity without the input, as a share of the stimulated maximum; EC50 shift
- **Gain of regulatory dependence** — The protein now requires an input that does not gate the wild-type protein, and is idle whenever that input is absent.
  - `dependent on:` `ligand`, `upstream kinase`, `upstream GTPase`, `cofactor`, `nucleotide`, `voltage`, `GEF/GAP`, `membrane recruitment`, `allosteric activator`, `proteolytic activation`
  - quantity, when reported: activity without the input, as a share of the stimulated maximum; EC50 shift
- **Folding destabilization** — Lowers the stability of the folded state.
  - `of:` `the whole protein`, `a domain`, `an interface`, `a local element`
  - quantity, when reported: ΔΔG of unfolding; ΔTm
- **Folding stabilization** — Raises the stability of the folded state.
  - `of:` `the whole protein`, `a domain`, `an interface`, `a local element`
  - quantity, when reported: ΔΔG of unfolding; ΔTm

### Chemistry and assembly

- **Gain of PTM site** — Creates a residue that becomes a substrate for a modifying enzyme.
  - `of:` `phosphorylation`, `glycosylation`, `ubiquitination`, `acetylation`, `methylation`, `lipidation`, `proteolytic cleavage`
  - quantity, when reported: modification stoichiometry or signal
- **Loss of PTM site** — Removes a regulatory modification the wild-type sequence carries.
  - `of:` `phosphorylation`, `glycosylation`, `ubiquitination`, `acetylation`, `methylation`, `lipidation`, `proteolytic cleavage`
  - quantity: none — leave `quantity` null
- **Gain of higher-order assembly** — The protein forms an assembly of indefinite stoichiometry the wild-type protein does not form, or forms more of it.
  - `into:` `condensate`, `amyloid fibril`, `amorphous aggregate`, `filament`
  - quantity, when reported: fraction assembled; saturation concentration
- **Loss of higher-order assembly** — Prevents an assembly of indefinite stoichiometry the wild-type protein forms.
  - `of:` `condensate`, `amyloid fibril`, `amorphous aggregate`, `filament`
  - quantity, when reported: fraction assembled; saturation concentration

### Location and amount

- **Change of localization** — Changes where the protein resides in the cell.
  - `by:` `loss of targeting signal`, `gain of targeting signal`, `membrane recruitment`, `release from anchor`
  - quantity, when reported: nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio; fraction at the membrane
- **Increased protein abundance** — Raises the steady-state amount of the protein without changing what one molecule does.
  - `by:` `loss of degron`, `increased stability`, `escape from proteolysis`
  - quantity, when reported: half-life; steady-state protein level
- **Decreased protein abundance** — Lowers the steady-state amount of the protein without changing what one molecule does.
  - `by:` `gain of degron`, `decreased stability`, `increased proteolysis`, `impaired maturation`
  - quantity, when reported: half-life; steady-state protein level

### Activity

- **Increase in intrinsic activity** — Each mutant molecule performs more of an activity the wild-type protein already has.
  - `of:` `catalytic`, `channel gating`, `transport`, `transcriptional`, `chromatin`, `effector engagement`, `motor`, `scaffolding`
  - quantity, when reported: kcat; kcat/Km; basal activity; open probability
- **Decrease in intrinsic activity** — Each mutant molecule performs less of an activity the wild-type protein already has, without abolishing it.
  - `of:` `catalytic`, `channel gating`, `transport`, `transcriptional`, `chromatin`, `effector engagement`, `motor`, `scaffolding`
  - quantity, when reported: kcat; kcat/Km; basal activity; open probability
- **Loss of an activity** — The mutant no longer performs an activity the wild-type protein has: the source reports it absent or at background. Reduced activity is a decrease, not a loss.
  - `of:` `catalytic`, `channel gating`, `transport`, `transcriptional`, `chromatin`, `effector engagement`, `motor`, `scaffolding`
  - quantity, when reported: residual activity, as a share of wild-type
- **Gain of a new activity** — The mutant performs an activity the wild-type protein does not have.
  - `as:` `new catalytic product`, `new substrate`, `new pathway coupling`, `new interaction-mediated function`
  - quantity, when reported: rate of the new activity
- **Gain of response to a non-cognate signal** — The protein is driven through its ordinary downstream pathway by an input the wild-type protein does not respond to.
  - `to:` `non-cognate ligand`, `antagonist`, `new activating partner`
  - quantity, when reported: EC50 of the new input; fold activation
- **Loss of response to a cognate signal** — The protein is no longer driven by an input the wild-type protein responds to: it stays idle when the input is present.
  - `to:` `cognate ligand`, `agonist`, `upstream activating partner`
  - quantity, when reported: EC50 shift; loss of stimulation
- **Interference with the wild-type protein** — The mutant lowers the activity of the wild-type protein it coexists with — the antimorphic effect.
  - `by:` `sequestration of a shared partner`, `poisoning of a hybrid complex`, `substrate trapping`
  - quantity, when reported: inhibition of the wild-type protein at a stated mutant:wild-type ratio

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
