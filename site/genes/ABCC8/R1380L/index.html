Wild type

ABCC8 encodes SUR1, the regulatory subunit of the pancreatic ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) channel, which co-assembles with the Kir6.2 pore-forming subunit. The channel links cellular metabolism to membrane excitability: ATP binds to Kir6.2 to close the pore, while Mg-nucleotide binding and MgATP hydrolysis at the two nucleotide-binding domains (NBD1 and NBD2) of SUR1 stimulate channel opening. Hydrolysis occurs at NBD2, and occupancy of the post-hydrolytic MgADP-bound state stabilizes an active conformation that antagonizes ATP inhibition. The highly conserved R1380 residue in NBD2 hydrogen-bonds with D860 in NBD1 to coordinate the post-hydrolytic inorganic phosphate, regulating its release and thus setting the rate of the catalytic cycle.

ABCC8 R1380L

The substituted leucine replaces a positively charged arginine with a neutral aliphatic side chain, abolishing the hydrogen bond with the NBD1 aspartate. Without this inter-domain interaction, the post-hydrolytic inorganic phosphate loses its local coordination, becomes rapidly hydrated, and is released more quickly from the catalytic site.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1 and candidate 3's complementary steps were merged to build the causal chain from molecular dynamics to cellular consequence. The substituted residue loses coordination of the inorganic phosphate, accelerating its release and increasing the overall MgATPase catalytic rate. This is biochemically supported by reduced trapping by the pre-hydrolytic analogue beryllium fluoride. The faster cycle shifts the conformational equilibrium of SUR1 toward the stimulatory MgADP-bound state, which in turn lowers the sensitivity of the assembled KATP channel to inhibitory ATP and increases the resting whole-cell potassium current. Candidate 2's abbreviated graph was dropped in favour of this detailed biochemical mechanism.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
