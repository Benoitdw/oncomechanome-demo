Wild type

Native ABL1 isoform 1b is held in an autoinhibited state by the insertion of its own myristoylated N-terminal cap into a deep hydrophobic pocket in the kinase domain's C-lobe, which bends the αI helix and allows the SH3 and SH2 domains to clamp the kinase shut. In the BCR::ABL1 fusion protein, the myristoylated cap is lost and the kinase is constitutively active. The allosteric drug asciminib mimics the missing myristoyl group, binding the empty myristoyl pocket to re-impose the bent-αI conformation and autoinhibitory clamp, shutting down the oncogenic kinase.

ABL1 V468F

Phenylalanine replaces valine 468, a residue that lines the floor of the myristoyl pocket. The bulkier aromatic side chain occupies the bottom of the cavity, pushing the allosteric ligand outward and decreasing the nonpolar contacts required for binding. Consequently, the mutant pocket can neither seat the physiological myristoyl group nor effectively accommodate asciminib.

Arbiter's reasoning

I merged the steps from all three candidates to build a unified mechanism. All candidates agree on the structural disruption of the myristoyl pocket, leading to two distinct branches. First, for the oncogenic fusion BCR::ABL1, the filled pocket reduces the binding affinity of the allosteric inhibitor asciminib, preventing it from inducing the inactive closed conformation, resulting in a loss of regulatory dependence on the drug and clinical resistance. I adopted candidate 1's 'Loss of regulatory dependence' as the most accurate label for this phenotype. Second, in the context of native ABL1 1b, the same pocket occlusion blocks the physiological myristoylated N-cap, lifting autoinhibition and intrinsically activating the kinase.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
