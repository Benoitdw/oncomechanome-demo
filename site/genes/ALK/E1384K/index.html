Wild type

Wild-type ALK is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally autoinhibited. Activation requires the binding of an extracellular ligand, which induces receptor dimerization. This event allows the intracellular kinase domains to trans-autophosphorylate at the activation loop and the C-terminal tail. The activated receptor then signals through downstream pathways including STAT3, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK/ERK to promote cell proliferation and survival.

ALK E1384K

The E1384K substitution replaces an electronegative glutamate with an electropositive lysine in the alpha-I helix of the kinase domain C-lobe. This charge reversal is proposed to induce electrostatic repulsion with R1231 in the adjacent alpha-E helix, disrupting the packing of the C-lobe without affecting the protein's overall stability or cytoplasmic localization.
