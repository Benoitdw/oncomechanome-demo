Wild type

Wild-type ALK is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally maintained in a low-activity, autoinhibited basal state. Its activation requires the binding of extracellular ALKAL-family ligands, which induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of the kinase domain. Gly1128 is the third glycine of the highly conserved, glycine-rich ATP-binding P-loop that caps the nucleotide-binding pocket.

ALK G1128A

The G1128A substitution replaces the third glycine of the P-loop with an alanine. The introduction of the methyl side chain at this normally flexible position alters the conformation and dynamics of the P-loop, relieving basal autoinhibition and predisposing the unphosphorylated kinase domain to an active state.

Arbiter's reasoning

The final graph merges the experimentally supported steps proposed by the candidates. The increase in intrinsic catalytic activity of the non-phosphorylated kinase domain and the resulting loss of regulatory dependence on ALKAL ligands are well-supported by biochemical and cellular evidence, and are included. Candidate 2's step capturing the variant's decreased affinity for the inhibitor crizotinib is also retained, supported by an increased IC50 in cellular assays and its clinical identification as an acquired resistance mutation. Candidate 1's step for increased ATP affinity is dropped because it relies on a K_M measurement rather than direct binding data. The conformational equilibrium shifts proposed by candidates 1 and 2 are excluded as they rest entirely on computational molecular dynamics simulations rather than experimental structural or biosensor evidence.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
