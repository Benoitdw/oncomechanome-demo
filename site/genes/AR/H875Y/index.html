Wild type

The androgen receptor (AR) is a ligand-activated transcription factor. Binding of androgens such as testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT) to its ligand-binding domain (LBD) induces a conformational change that repositions helix 12 to form the activation function 2 (AF2) surface. This surface mediates the N/C interaction by binding the receptor's own N-terminal FXXLF motif, and recruits p160 coactivators via their LXXLL motifs to drive transcription of androgen-response elements. Wild-type AR is essentially inactive in the absence of hormone or in the presence of adrenal androgens, progesterone, and estradiol, and is effectively inhibited by antiandrogens.

AR H875Y

Tyrosine replaces histidine 875 (historically numbered H874) at the C-terminal end of LBD helix 10/11. The bulkier tyrosine side chain does not line the steroid pocket but sits in the cavity between helices 11 and 12, replacing a water-mediated hydrogen bond network with direct hydrogen bonds to core residues such as Tyr-739 in helix 4. This stabilizes the interior core and strengthens the packing of helix 12 in the active agonist conformation without significantly altering the affinity for DHT.
