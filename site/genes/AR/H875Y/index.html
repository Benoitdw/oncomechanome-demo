Wild type

The androgen receptor (AR) is a ligand-activated transcription factor. Binding of androgens such as testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT) to its ligand-binding domain (LBD) induces a conformational change that repositions helix 12 to form the activation function 2 (AF2) surface. This surface mediates the N/C interaction by binding the receptor's own N-terminal FXXLF motif, and recruits p160 coactivators via their LXXLL motifs to drive transcription of androgen-response elements. Wild-type AR is essentially inactive in the absence of hormone or in the presence of adrenal androgens, progesterone, and estradiol, and is effectively inhibited by antiandrogens.

AR H875Y

Tyrosine replaces histidine 875 (historically numbered H874) at the C-terminal end of LBD helix 10/11. The bulkier tyrosine side chain does not line the steroid pocket but sits in the cavity between helices 11 and 12, replacing a water-mediated hydrogen bond network with direct hydrogen bonds to core residues such as Tyr-739 in helix 4. This stabilizes the interior core and strengthens the packing of helix 12 in the active agonist conformation without significantly altering the affinity for DHT.

Arbiter's reasoning

Steps from both candidates were merged, preserving the extensive list of non-cognate signals and antiandrogen conversions correctly identified by both. Candidate 2's detailed structural steps—core stabilization leading to improved binding of testosterone, the N-terminal FXXLF motif, and p160 coactivators—were included. As required by the schema, Candidate 2's bundled step for p160 coactivators was split into one step per specific coactivator (SRC-1a, TIF2, AIB1). Candidate 2's inclusion of apo LBD destabilization, 11-ketotestosterone sensitization, and ligand-dependent interactome rewiring were verified and retained. Missing PMC IDs were replaced with the correct resolvable identifiers (e.g. PMC4729137 for Lallous et al.). Edges were set to reflect that core stabilization enhances specific AF2 interactions and slows testosterone dissociation, which all converge on increased intrinsic transactivation, while the conformational shift enables constitutive activity and promiscuous responses to multiple alternative ligands.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
