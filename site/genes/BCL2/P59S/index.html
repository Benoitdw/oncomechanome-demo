Not covered by the vocabulary

Although P59S is a recurrent somatic mutation in follicular lymphoma and chronic lymphocytic leukemia, typically arising through activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AICDA) hypermutation on translocated alleles, the retrieved literature lacks variant-specific functional assays demonstrating its direct molecular consequences. Studies report its occurrence and provide in silico predictions, but do not provide experimental evidence for how the substitution alters BCL-2's binding affinities, conformational dynamics, stability, or anti-apoptotic function. Because no downstream molecular event caused by this specific substitution can be established from the cited literature, the variant's mechanistic role remains undetermined.

This is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of the vocabulary.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates' conclusion that the variant is uncovered was accepted. The literature establishes P59S as a recurrent mutation in B-cell lymphomas, likely resulting from AID-mediated somatic hypermutation. However, the retrieved sources only report its clinical occurrence or provide in silico predictions. While adjacent loop mutations have been assayed, no study directly measures the functional consequences of the P59S substitution on BCL-2 binding, stability, or anti-apoptotic activity. Therefore, no steps were generated.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
