Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a RAS-regulated serine/threonine kinase in the MAPK cascade. In resting cells, it exists as an autoinhibited monomer in which the N-terminal regulatory region (containing the Ras-binding and cysteine-rich domains) folds back onto the kinase domain to restrict its activity. Activation requires GTP-bound RAS to recruit BRAF to the plasma membrane, relieving this autoinhibition and promoting side-to-side dimerization of the kinase domain (either homodimerization or heterodimerization with CRAF). Within the active dimer, the aspartate of the conserved DFG motif (Asp594) coordinates a catalytic magnesium ion to position ATP for phosphotransfer, allowing the active protomer to phosphorylate downstream MEK. Output is subsequently limited by negative feedback, including an inhibitory autophosphorylation that disrupts the RAF dimers.

BRAF D594G

Glycine replaces the highly conserved Asp594, the critical aspartate of the DFG motif at the start of the activation segment. The substitution removes the carboxylate side chain necessary for chelating the catalytic magnesium and stabilizing ATP. While rendering the kinase domain catalytically dead, the lack of a bound nucleotide and the loss of the aspartate side chain allow the activation loop to extend and the αC-helix to rotate into the IN position. This places the kinase domain in a partially active, dimerization-competent conformation while weakening its intramolecular contact with the N-terminal regulatory region.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the primary mechanisms of D594G: loss of magnesium and ATP binding leads to a catalytically dead kinase domain, while the empty nucleotide pocket and loss of the DFG aspartate favor a partially active conformation. This conformation escapes the N-terminal autoinhibitory clamp, enhancing binding to both RAS and CRAF. The steps were consolidated into a single coherent graph. Candidate 3's addition of steps for the loss of N-terminal binding and increased kinase homodimerization were included, as they distinguish the intramolecular and intermolecular structural shifts. Candidate 1 and 3's explicit step for the gain of scaffolding function was retained over Candidate 2's interactome rewiring step, as it captures the specific functional consequence (allosteric transactivation of CRAF) of the heterodimer. Candidate 2 and 3's step for increased protein stability within the complex was also included. Edges were merged to reflect that loss of autoinhibition and the active-like conformation synergistically drive RAS and CRAF engagement.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
