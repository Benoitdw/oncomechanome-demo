Wild type

BRAF is a RAS-regulated serine/threonine kinase in the MAPK pathway. In the absence of active RAS, wild-type BRAF exists as an autoinhibited monomer bound to MEK and 14-3-3 proteins. Upon RAS-GTP binding, it is recruited to the membrane, releases autoinhibition, and dimerizes. Within the active dimer, the highly conserved DFG motif at the start of the activation segment—where Asp594 is located—chelates the catalytic Mg2+ ion necessary to coordinate ATP and phosphorylate MEK1/2.

BRAF D594Y

The D594Y substitution replaces the catalytic DFG aspartate with a bulky aromatic tyrosine, removing the carboxylate group required for Mg2+ coordination. This destroys the kinase's ability to transfer a phosphate to MEK. However, the loss of this interaction at the active site destabilizes the autoinhibited monomeric state and shifts the kinase domain toward a conformation that favors dimerization.
