Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a RAS-regulated serine/threonine kinase of the MAPK cascade that phosphorylates MEK1/2. In the resting state, the kinase domain is held inactive through a hydrophobic association between the glycine-rich P-loop and the activation segment. Activation normally requires GTP-loaded RAS to bind the regulatory domains, recruit BRAF to the plasma membrane, and promote side-to-side RAF dimerization, which relieves the autoinhibited conformation.

BRAF G464R

Arginine replaces glycine 464, the first conserved glycine of the kinase-domain glycine-rich P-loop. The introduction of a bulky, positively charged side chain is incompatible with the close hydrophobic packing between the P-loop and the activation segment that maintains the wild-type kinase in its inactive state.

These structural changes enable constitutive, RAS-independent dimerization, leading to elevated catalytic activity. This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
