Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase that links RAS-GTP activation to the MEK/ERK signalling cascade. In the resting state, it is maintained in an autoinhibited conformation. Upon growth factor stimulation, activated RAS-GTP recruits BRAF to the plasma membrane, where it forms side-to-side dimers. This dimerization enables the kinase domain to directly phosphorylate MEK1/2. Catalytic activity depends on a highly conserved DFG (Asp-Phe-Gly) motif at the base of the activation segment, in which the aspartate residue is required to position ATP for phosphotransfer.

BRAF G596R

The G596R substitution replaces the flexible glycine of the conserved DFG motif with a bulky, positively charged arginine. Molecular dynamics simulations indicate that this arginine forms a stable hydrogen bond with the catalytic Asp594, pulling it out of the geometry required to coordinate ATP. This structural disruption misaligns the active site, severely impairing the intrinsic kinase activity of the mutant protein, while leaving the RAS-binding domain and the dimer interface intact.

Arbiter's reasoning

The mechanism was built primarily from candidate_3's graph structure and evidence, which correctly establish the sequence of events defined by Yao et al. for class 3 BRAF mutants. Catalytic impairment directly leads to tighter RAS-GTP binding, which in turn drives heterodimerization with CRAF and shifts pathway output onto these complexes. The proposed step for ATP binding decrease (candidate_2) was not included, as the primary effect on the active site is catalytic impairment, which sufficiently covers the outcome. A conflicting report of RAS-independent CRAF binding (candidate_2) was noted but omitted from the graph to maintain the coherent RAS-dependent mechanism supported by the bulk of the recent literature. The transactivation of CRAF is encapsulated in the interactome rewiring step rather than recorded as a separate increase in intrinsic activity.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
