Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase that functions in the MAPK signalling cascade. In its inactive state, its kinase domain is autoinhibited. Upon binding to active, GTP-loaded RAS at the plasma membrane, BRAF dimerises and its activation segment adopts an active conformation. The active dimer phosphorylates MEK1/2, which in turn activates ERK. Wild-type BRAF can also form functional heterodimers with CRAF.

BRAF G596V

The G596V substitution replaces the highly conserved glycine of the DFG motif in the kinase activation segment with a bulkier valine. This structural change restricts the conformational flexibility required for the activation loop to adopt a fully catalytically competent state, intrinsically crippling the variant's own kinase activity while preserving its ability to bind RAS and form RAF dimers.
