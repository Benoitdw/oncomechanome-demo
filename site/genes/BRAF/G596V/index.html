Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase that functions in the MAPK signalling cascade. In its inactive state, its kinase domain is autoinhibited. Upon binding to active, GTP-loaded RAS at the plasma membrane, BRAF dimerises and its activation segment adopts an active conformation. The active dimer phosphorylates MEK1/2, which in turn activates ERK. Wild-type BRAF can also form functional heterodimers with CRAF.

BRAF G596V

The G596V substitution replaces the highly conserved glycine of the DFG motif in the kinase activation segment with a bulkier valine. This structural change restricts the conformational flexibility required for the activation loop to adopt a fully catalytically competent state, intrinsically crippling the variant's own kinase activity while preserving its ability to bind RAS and form RAF dimers.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1 recorded the reduced catalytic activity but omitted the mechanism by which the variant signals in vivo. Candidates 2 and 3 correctly captured the class III mechanism. Candidate 2's steps for tighter binding to RAS-GTP, tighter binding to CRAF, and enhanced scaffolding of CRAF were retained, as they accurately describe the molecular consequences of the mutation. Candidate 2's 'Gain of regulatory dependence' step was dropped because wild-type BRAF is already dependent on upstream RAS, meaning there is no gain of dependence relative to the wild-type state. Candidate 3's 'Gain of a new activity' was replaced by Candidate 2's 'Increase in intrinsic activity' (scaffolding), since wild-type BRAF also transactivates CRAF, but the mutant does so more effectively.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
