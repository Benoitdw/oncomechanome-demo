Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase that functions as a central MAP3K in the MAPK signaling cascade. In quiescent cells, the protein is held in an autoinhibited monomeric state through interactions between its N-terminal regulatory regions and the kinase domain. Activation requires the recruitment of BRAF to the plasma membrane by active RAS-GTP, followed by side-to-side dimerization of the kinase domains. This dimerization shifts the regulatory αC-helix into an active inward conformation, enabling the kinase to phosphorylate its physiological substrates, MEK1 and MEK2, and propagate the signal to ERK.

BRAF K499E

The substitution replaces a conserved, positively charged lysine with a negatively charged glutamate at position 499, a residue located on the regulatory αC-helix of the kinase domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 3's conformational equilibrium shift step was dropped because it was inferred from substitutions to other residues (such as K499L) rather than measured for the K499E mutant itself, as the candidate acknowledged. The remaining steps for increased intrinsic catalytic activity and loss of regulatory dependence on RAS-GTP were kept and merged from Candidates 1 and 3. Candidate 2 proposed only the increase in catalytic activity and was merged into that step. The loss of regulatory dependence rests on the mutant's database classification as a class II BRAF variant, which by definition signals independently of RAS.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
