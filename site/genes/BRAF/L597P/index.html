Not covered by the vocabulary

No variant-specific primary evidence exists for BRAF L597P. The literature explicitly notes that "L597P has not been functionally or clinically validated, but is considered likely oncogenic as a result of its vicinity to L597Q/R/S" and that "its effect on Braf protein function is unknown". While reviews assign L597P to the class II BRAF mutants (which are RAS-independent, dimer-dependent, and possess intermediate-to-high kinase activity), they do so by grouping it with functionally characterized substitutions at the same codon (L597Q, L597R, L597S, L597V) rather than from direct biochemical assays of the proline allele.

This is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of the vocabulary.

Arbiter's reasoning

Both candidate_1 and candidate_3 correctly recognized that there is no primary biochemical evidence for the L597P substitution, pointing to multiple sources explicitly stating it has not been functionally validated. candidate_2's steps were rejected because they relied entirely on inference from neighbouring substitutions (L597Q/R/S) and unmeasured structural predictions for the proline residue, which violates the requirement that every step must be supported by a direct observation from a cited source. The variant is therefore annotated as uncovered.

Rejected in whole: candidate_2

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
