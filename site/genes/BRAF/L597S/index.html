Wild type

Wild-type BRAF is a RAS-regulated serine/threonine kinase that functions as a central node in the MAPK signaling cascade. In the resting state, its kinase domain is maintained in an autoinhibited conformation through an intramolecular hydrophobic network where Leu597 on the activation loop and Val600 on the AS-H1 helix pack against the beta-3 strand and P-loop. Activation normally requires binding to GTP-loaded RAS at the plasma membrane, which relieves N-terminal autoinhibition and enables the kinase domains to assemble into active side-by-side dimers that phosphorylate MEK1 and MEK2.

BRAF L597S

The L597S substitution replaces a buried, aliphatic leucine residue with a small, polar serine, disrupting the hydrophobic network that pins the activation segment against the P-loop. The loss of this packing element releases the kinase domain from its inactive conformation, rendering it primed for constitutive dimerization.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
