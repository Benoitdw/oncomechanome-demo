Wild type

BRAF is a serine/threonine kinase that functions as a core component of the MAPK signaling cascade, where it phosphorylates and activates MEK1/2. In its basal state, the BRAF kinase domain is held in an inactive, autoinhibited conformation through an intramolecular interaction in which the hydrophobic side chain of Val600 in the activation segment is buried in a pocket formed by the P-loop, and the protein is bound to 14-3-3 proteins. Activation typically requires RAS-GTP-mediated recruitment to the plasma membrane, side-to-side dimerization of the kinase domain, and phosphorylation of the activation segment at Thr599 and Ser602 to release the autoinhibitory contacts and stabilize the catalytically active state.

BRAF V600E

The substitution replaces the hydrophobic valine with a larger, negatively charged glutamate that sterically and electrostatically clashes with the hydrophobic pocket, preventing the activation segment from packing against the P-loop. The introduced glutamate acts as a phosphomimetic, forming a novel salt bridge with Lys507 in the αC-helix that constitutively locks the kinase domain in an active conformation with an inward-shifted αC-helix and an extended activation loop.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates provided complementary and highly congruent descriptions of the V600E mechanism, converging on the disruption of autoinhibition and a shift to an active kinase conformation. Candidate 3's broader graph was used as the scaffold to capture both the catalytic changes and the interactome rewiring. The steps detailing independence from upstream kinase, GTPase, membrane recruitment, and allosteric activation were included, reflecting the mutant's ability to signal autonomously as a monomer. Intrinsic activity and dimerization propensity steps were retained and quantified using standard literature estimates. Candidate 3's steps for the unique chaperone dependence (HSP90/CDC37) and 14-3-3 dissociation were refined: HSP90 was modeled as an increase in binding affinity rather than a strictly new interface, given recent structural evidence that wild-type BRAF is a weak client. Candidate 2's specific 'Folding destabilization' of the AS-H1 helix was omitted, as sources emphasize the overall kinase fold remains intact.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
