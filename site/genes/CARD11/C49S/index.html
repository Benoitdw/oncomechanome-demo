Wild type

CARD11 is a lymphocyte-restricted scaffolding protein that couples antigen-receptor signaling to NF-κB activation. In resting cells, it is held in a closed, inactive conformation by autoinhibitory intramolecular interactions between its internal inhibitory domain (ID) and the CARD and coiled-coil domains. Upon antigen-receptor engagement, PKC-mediated phosphorylation of the ID relieves this autoinhibition, allowing the CARD domain to recruit BCL10 and assemble a complex that activates IKK and NF-κB.

CARD11 C49S

The C49S substitution replaces a cysteine with a serine in the N-terminal CARD domain. This alters the CARD-ID interface, slightly weakening the autoinhibitory binding of the inhibitory domain in trans and exposing the CARD domain to recruit BCL10 in the absence of upstream signaling.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
