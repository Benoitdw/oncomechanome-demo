Wild type

CARD11 is a scaffold protein expressed in lymphocytes that links antigen receptor signaling to NF-κB activation. In resting cells, the protein is held in a closed, autoinhibited conformation through intramolecular contacts between its CARD, LATCH, coiled-coil, and inhibitory domains. Upon antigen receptor engagement, upstream kinases phosphorylate the inhibitory domain, relieving this autoinhibition. This allows CARD11 to open, assemble into oligomeric complexes, and recruit BCL10 and MALT1 to form the CBM signalosome, which subsequently drives downstream IKK and NF-κB activation.

CARD11 T117P

The substitution replaces a conserved threonine with a rigid proline within the LATCH domain. This mutation disrupts the local intramolecular contacts essential for maintaining the autoinhibited state, forcing the protein into a constitutively active conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates generally agree on the mechanism, describing a loss of autoinhibition that leads to spontaneous aggregation and constitutive signaling. Candidate 1's steps for 'Loss of regulatory dependence' (independent of ligand and independent of upstream kinase) were retained as both upstream layers (receptor and BTK kinase) are bypassed. Candidate 2's 'Conformational equilibrium shift' was dropped as it describes the same structural transition as 'Loss of autoinhibition' and relies on general class evidence rather than T117P-specific data. The steps were merged and reordered to show that the disrupted intramolecular contact (s1) leads to stimulus independence (s2, s3) and spontaneous higher-order assembly (s4), which together drive the increased scaffolding activity (s5).

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
