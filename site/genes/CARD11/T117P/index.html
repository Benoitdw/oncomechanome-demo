Wild type

CARD11 is a scaffold protein expressed in lymphocytes that links antigen receptor signaling to NF-κB activation. In resting cells, the protein is held in a closed, autoinhibited conformation through intramolecular contacts between its CARD, LATCH, coiled-coil, and inhibitory domains. Upon antigen receptor engagement, upstream kinases phosphorylate the inhibitory domain, relieving this autoinhibition. This allows CARD11 to open, assemble into oligomeric complexes, and recruit BCL10 and MALT1 to form the CBM signalosome, which subsequently drives downstream IKK and NF-κB activation.

CARD11 T117P

The substitution replaces a conserved threonine with a rigid proline within the LATCH domain. This mutation disrupts the local intramolecular contacts essential for maintaining the autoinhibited state, forcing the protein into a constitutively active conformation.
