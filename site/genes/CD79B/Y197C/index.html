Wild type

CD79B forms a disulfide-linked heterodimer with CD79A to provide the signal-transducing component of the B-cell receptor (BCR). Its cytoplasmic domain contains an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM) bearing two tyrosines. Upon antigen binding, SRC-family kinases such as LYN phosphorylate both ITAM tyrosines, creating a docking site for SYK to initiate downstream signaling cascades including the NF-κB pathway. Additionally, in its unphosphorylated state, the membrane-proximal first ITAM tyrosine is the critical invariant residue of a YxxØ motif that binds the AP2 clathrin adaptor complex to mediate constitutive and ligand-induced BCR endocytosis. LYN kinase also acts as a negative regulator of BCR signaling by promoting BCR internalization and recruiting inhibitory phosphatases.

CD79B Y197C

Cysteine replaces the membrane-proximal first ITAM tyrosine (Y197, heavily cited as Y196 in the literature) in the CD79B cytoplasmic tail, simultaneously deleting the critical phosphoacceptor site for SRC-family kinases and the core invariant residue of the AP2-binding endocytic motif.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
