Wild type

β-catenin is the principal transcriptional co-activator of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway and also functions in cell-cell adhesion at the membrane. In the absence of Wnt ligands, cytoplasmic β-catenin levels are kept low by a destruction complex comprising APC, Axin, CK1α, and GSK3β. CK1α phosphorylates β-catenin at Ser45, priming sequential phosphorylation by GSK3β at Thr41, Ser37, and Ser33. The dual phosphorylation of Ser33 and Ser37 completes the DpSGXXpS degron motif (residues 32–37), which serves as a high-affinity docking site for the WD40 β-propeller of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase receptor β-TRCP. β-TRCP binding targets β-catenin for ubiquitylation and rapid proteasomal degradation. Wnt pathway activation inhibits this destruction complex, allowing β-catenin to accumulate, translocate to the nucleus, and activate TCF/LEF-dependent target gene transcription.

CTNNB1 S33P

The S33P substitution replaces the final GSK3β phosphoacceptor serine of the N-terminal degron with a proline. Proline lacks a hydroxyl group and cannot be phosphorylated, so the mutant protein can only generate an incompletely phosphorylated degron, eliminating key polar contacts normally made between the Ser33 phosphate and the β-TRCP receptor.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates correctly describe how S33P destroys a GSK3β phosphorylation site required for β-TRCP binding, causing the protein to escape proteasomal degradation and accumulate to drive TCF/LEF transcription. I merged their steps, selecting 'Decrease binding affinity' for the β-TRCP interaction rather than 'Loss' to reflect candidate_2's retrieved >10 μM Kd bound. Candidate_1's 'Change of localization' was dropped; the vocabulary's slot values do not adequately describe simple escape from cytoplasmic destruction, and nuclear accumulation is effectively the premise for the subsequent 'Increase in intrinsic activity (of: transcriptional)'. The 'Loss of regulatory dependence' on GSK3β proposed by candidate_1 and candidate_2 was also omitted, as it is conceptually redundant with the loss of the specific phosphorylation site and subsequent escape from proteolysis.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
