Wild type

β-catenin acts both as a structural component of adherens junctions and as the central transcriptional co-activator in the canonical Wnt signaling pathway. In the absence of Wnt, its N-terminal degron is phosphorylated sequentially by CK1α and GSK3β, targeting it for proteasomal degradation. Independent of this destruction cascade, Wnt3a stimulation triggers the phosphorylation of serine 47 by CK1δ. This specific p-S47 modification allows β-catenin to bind the motor proteins KIF11 and KIFC3, driving its transport from the Golgi apparatus to the centrosome to promote primary ciliogenesis.

CTNNB1 S47R

The substitution replaces the critical polar serine 47 with a bulky, positively charged arginine. This physically eliminates the CK1δ phosphorylation site, preventing the mutant protein from forming the p-S47 epitope required for its non-canonical role in primary ciliogenesis.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
