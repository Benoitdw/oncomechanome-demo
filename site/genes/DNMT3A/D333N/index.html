Wild type

DNMT3A is a de novo DNA methyltransferase that deposits methylation on CpG dinucleotides. Its N-terminal PWWP domain recognizes dimethylated and trimethylated histone H3 lysine 36 (H3K36me2/3), which targets the enzyme to H3K36-methylated intergenic and gene-body chromatin while keeping it away from Polycomb-regulated CpG islands. The PWWP domain also mediates intramolecular autoinhibition by docking onto the ADD domain, occluding the enzyme's active site until histone binding relieves this restraint. Additionally, an N-terminal ubiquitin-dependent recruitment region can bind H2AK119ub, though the PWWP-H3K36 interaction normally outcompetes this targeting mechanism.

DNMT3A D333N

The D333N substitution replaces the negatively charged aspartate residue in the PWWP domain's aromatic cage with an uncharged asparagine. This residue normally coordinates both the positively charged methyl-lysine of H3K36 and a corresponding arginine from the ADD domain during autoinhibition.

Arbiter's reasoning

Merged steps from all three candidates to construct a complete mechanism. I retained the loss of H3K36me2 and H3K36me3 binding (all candidates) and the corresponding loss of normal targeting to intergenic regions (candidate_2). The D333N mutation also reduces the PWWP–ADD domain intramolecular interaction (candidate_2, candidate_3), which disrupts autoinhibition (all candidates) and increases intrinsic catalytic activity (all candidates). Furthermore, an increased affinity for DNA is retained (all candidates). Following loss of normal H3K36me2 anchoring, the mutant enzyme mistargets to H2AK119ub-enriched Polycomb-regulated regions (candidate_2), leading to the aberrant de novo hypermethylation of these regions, modeled as a gain of a new activity (candidate_1). Candidate 1's interactome rewiring step was dropped as it is a consequence of the binding and localization changes. Candidate 2's increase in binding affinity for H2AK119ub was replaced by the change in localization step, as the UDR affinity is unchanged but competition is lost.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
