Wild type

DNMT3A is a de novo DNA methyltransferase that deposits 5-methylcytosine at CpG dinucleotides. Its PWWP domain reads di- and tri-methylated histone H3 lysine 36 (H3K36me2 and H3K36me3) through an aromatic cage, which targets the enzyme to H3K36me2-rich intergenic chromatin and away from CpG islands. The PWWP domain also mediates autoinhibition by packing against the ADD and methyltransferase domains, occluding both the H3K36me2 pocket and the CpG-recognition loop. Full catalytic activity requires release of this autoinhibition by H3K36me2 and H3K4me0. The DNMT3A1 isoform additionally contains a separate N-terminal ubiquitin-dependent recruitment (UDR) region that can bind H2AK119ub-marked nucleosomes, providing a targeting mechanism that normally competes with PWWP-mediated recruitment.

DNMT3A W330R

The substitution replaces a neutral tryptophan with a positively charged arginine (W330R) in the PWWP domain. Tryptophan 330 forms one wall of the aromatic cage that encages methylated H3K36 and also sits in the intramolecular interface between the PWWP and ADD domains. This substitution destroys the methyl-lysine aromatic cage, weakens the intramolecular PWWP-ADD contact, and increases the net positive charge of the PWWP surface, raising its non-specific affinity for DNA.

Arbiter's reasoning

All candidates successfully captured the primary molecular events: loss of binding to H3K36me2/3, increased non-specific DNA binding, impaired PWWP-ADD domain interactions, relief of autoinhibition, and increased basal catalytic activity. Steps were merged to construct a single pathway where the biochemical changes drive the relocalization of the mutant enzyme from H3K36me2-rich regions to H2AK119ub-marked Polycomb CpG islands and a resulting interactome rewiring. The step capturing decreased binding affinity with self (the ADD domain) was extracted from candidates 2 and 3 to distinguish it from the downstream functional loss of autoinhibition. The in silico interaction destabilization quantity for H3K36me3 was retained from candidates 1 and 2, as it accurately reflects the cited source.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
