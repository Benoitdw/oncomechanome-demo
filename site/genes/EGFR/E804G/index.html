Wild type

The epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a single-pass transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase. In the absence of ligand, it is held in an inactive state. Binding of an extracellular ligand, such as epidermal growth factor, drives receptor dimerization and the formation of an asymmetric kinase domain dimer. This activates the intracellular kinase, leading to autophosphorylation of C-terminal tyrosine residues and the recruitment of adaptor proteins that initiate downstream signaling pathways, including MAPK, AKT, and STAT3.

EGFR E804G

The E804G substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a small, uncharged glycine at position 804 in the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. This deletes the acidic side chain and increases local backbone flexibility.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
