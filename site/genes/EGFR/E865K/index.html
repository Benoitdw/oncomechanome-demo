Wild type

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that resides in an autoinhibited state at the plasma membrane. Binding of extracellular ligands, such as EGF, induces receptor dimerization. This interaction drives asymmetric dimerization of the intracellular kinase domains and allosterically displaces the autoinhibitory activation segment, leading to catalytic activation and autophosphorylation.

EGFR E865K

The E865K substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged lysine at position 865, reversing the local charge within the activation segment of the kinase domain.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
