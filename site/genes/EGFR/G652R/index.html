Wild type

EGFR is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that is largely inactive in its monomeric resting state. Binding of epidermal growth factor (EGF) to the extracellular domain drives receptor homodimerization, which is transmitted across the lipid bilayer by the single transmembrane helix to promote the formation of an asymmetric, active intracellular kinase dimer. This results in the autophosphorylation of C-terminal tyrosine residues, which recruit downstream signaling effectors.

EGFR G652R

The G652R substitution replaces a small hydrophobic glycine in the transmembrane helix with a bulky, positively charged arginine. Molecular dynamics simulations indicate that while the long arginine side chain can snorkel toward the hydrophilic lipid head groups to accommodate the charge within the bilayer, its presence at the helix-helix interface introduces electrostatic repulsion.
