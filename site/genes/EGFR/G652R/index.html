Wild type

EGFR is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that is largely inactive in its monomeric resting state. Binding of epidermal growth factor (EGF) to the extracellular domain drives receptor homodimerization, which is transmitted across the lipid bilayer by the single transmembrane helix to promote the formation of an asymmetric, active intracellular kinase dimer. This results in the autophosphorylation of C-terminal tyrosine residues, which recruit downstream signaling effectors.

EGFR G652R

The G652R substitution replaces a small hydrophobic glycine in the transmembrane helix with a bulky, positively charged arginine. Molecular dynamics simulations indicate that while the long arginine side chain can snorkel toward the hydrophilic lipid head groups to accommodate the charge within the bilayer, its presence at the helix-helix interface introduces electrostatic repulsion.

Arbiter's reasoning

All candidates correctly identified that G652R causes ligand-independent activation driven by an increase in basal catalytic activity per molecule. I merged candidate_1's separate N-terminal and C-terminal dimerization steps into a single transmembrane self-association step as proposed by candidate_3, since both dimer poses are similarly disfavored by electrostatic repulsion. I dropped candidate_1's membrane binding step, as the simulated free energy of insertion reflects how the mutated side chain is thermodynamically accommodated in the bilayer rather than a functional gain of membrane recruitment; this structural adaptation is described in the substitution effect instead. Candidate 2 was missing the self-association step entirely.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
