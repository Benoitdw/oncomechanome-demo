Wild type

Wild-type EGFR is a receptor tyrosine kinase that resides in an inactive, autoinhibited state when unliganded. In this state, the regulatory αC-helix of the kinase domain is rotated outward, stabilized by hydrophobic packing that includes the flexible, glycine-rich phosphate-binding loop (P-loop) arching over the ATP-binding cleft. Catalytic activation is gated by the binding of an extracellular EGF-family ligand, which promotes receptor dimerization. Dimerization induces an asymmetric interaction between the kinase domains, pushing the receiver's αC-helix inward to the active conformation required for substrate phosphorylation and downstream signaling.

EGFR G719C

The G719C substitution replaces the first glycine of the conserved GXGXXG motif in the P-loop with a bulkier cysteine. This non-glycine side chain restricts the backbone flexibility of the P-loop, altering its local geometry and perturbing the hydrophobic packing that normally restrains the αC-helix in its inactive, outward-rotated position.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
