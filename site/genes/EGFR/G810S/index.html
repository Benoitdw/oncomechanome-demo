Wild type

EGFR is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally gated by the binding of epidermal growth factor (EGF) and related extracellular ligands. Ligand binding induces receptor dimerization and allosteric kinase activation, followed by autophosphorylation and the initiation of downstream proliferation and survival signaling cascades.

EGFR G810S

The G810S substitution replaces a small, non-polar glycine with a polar, hydroxyl-bearing serine residue in the exon 20-encoded region of the EGFR tyrosine kinase domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1's steps were rejected: its quote could not be matched to the cited source (PMID 34260383, which is a paper on bird migration), and it confused EGFR G810S with the well-characterised RET G810S solvent-front resistance mutation. Candidate 2 correctly identified the sole primary study profiling EGFR G810S (Kancha et al., 2009) and proposed valid steps for ligand-independent activation and resistance to gefitinib and erlotinib, which were retained. Candidate 3's single step for loss of regulatory dependence was merged. 'Decrease binding affinity' was kept for the drug resistance steps, noting that the underlying measurements are cellular proliferation IC50s rather than direct binding constants.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
