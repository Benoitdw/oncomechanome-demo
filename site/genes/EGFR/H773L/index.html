Wild type

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated when ligand binding drives dimerization, pushing the kinase domain αC-helix inward to a catalytically active position. In the inactive monomeric state, the αC-helix is rotated outward. Within the loop following this helix (the αC-β4 loop), the side chain of His773 donates a hydrogen bond to the backbone carbonyl oxygen of Val851 in the C-lobe. This tether anchors the loop, helping stabilise the inactive arrangement of the kinase domain and the architecture of the adjacent catalytic cleft.

EGFR H773L

The substitution replaces the hydrogen-bond-donating histidine with an aliphatic leucine, which cannot act as a hydrogen-bond donor. This eliminates the intramolecular tether between the αC-β4 loop and Val851, loosening the local conformation of the loop and relieving the structural restraint that stabilises the inactive αC-out state. Consequently, the αC-helix is permitted to shift toward the active, inward position without requiring an activating ligand.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1 correctly identified the conformational shift but missed the upstream and downstream mechanism. Candidates 2 and 3 independently provided detailed, congruent chains spanning from the loss of the local hydrogen bond to loop destabilization, the conformational shift of the αC-helix, catalytic activation, and resistance to lapatinib and CL-387,785. Candidate 3's use of 'Loss of autoinhibition of: intramolecular contact' was preferred over Candidate 2's 'Loss of binding affinity with: self', as it more accurately describes the release of a structural restraint. The steps were otherwise merged, combining evidence and preserving Candidate 2's quantitative measurement of CL-387,785 resistance to build a unified graph linking structural loosening to constitutive activity and drug resistance.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
