Wild type

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated when ligand binding drives dimerization, pushing the kinase domain αC-helix inward to a catalytically active position. In the inactive monomeric state, the αC-helix is rotated outward. Within the loop following this helix (the αC-β4 loop), the side chain of His773 donates a hydrogen bond to the backbone carbonyl oxygen of Val851 in the C-lobe. This tether anchors the loop, helping stabilise the inactive arrangement of the kinase domain and the architecture of the adjacent catalytic cleft.

EGFR H773L

The substitution replaces the hydrogen-bond-donating histidine with an aliphatic leucine, which cannot act as a hydrogen-bond donor. This eliminates the intramolecular tether between the αC-β4 loop and Val851, loosening the local conformation of the loop and relieving the structural restraint that stabilises the inactive αC-out state. Consequently, the αC-helix is permitted to shift toward the active, inward position without requiring an activating ligand.
