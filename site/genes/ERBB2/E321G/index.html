Wild type

ERBB2 (HER2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase of the epidermal growth factor (EGF) receptor family. Although it lacks a known cognate ligand, its extracellular domain exists in a conformation that readily heterodimerizes with ligand-bound EGFR, HER3, or HER4. Dimerization brings the intracellular kinase domains together, enabling trans-autophosphorylation and subsequent downstream signaling pathways that promote cell proliferation. The extracellular subdomain II participates in receptor dimerization and is stabilized by 11 intramolecular disulfide bonds and internal salt bridges, including one formed between E321 and K369/R434.

ERBB2 E321G

The substitution of glutamate for glycine at position 321 removes the acidic side chain required for salt bridge formation with K369 and R434. This disrupts the stabilizing intramolecular contacts in extracellular subdomain II, loosening the structure of its disulfide-bonded loops and freeing cysteines that normally form intramolecular bonds.

Arbiter's reasoning

The graph merges the steps from the candidates to describe folding destabilization (s1), subsequent covalent homodimerization (s2), and the resulting kinase activation (s3). Candidates 1 and 3's step for folding destabilization was included, as the E321-K369/R434 salt bridge disruption is the literature-proposed mechanism for the resulting aberrant disulfide bonding. The step denoting covalent intermolecular dimerization was included from all candidates. Candidate 1's 'Loss of regulatory dependence' and Candidate 3's 'Gain of a new activity' were excluded because cell transformation and IL-3 independence are cellular outcomes rather than molecular events, and wild-type ERBB2 already functions without a direct cognate ligand.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
