Wild type

HER2 (ERBB2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family. Unlike other family members, its extracellular domain constitutively adopts an open conformation without binding a known ligand. Instead, it serves as the preferred dimerization partner for other ligand-bound ERBB receptors. Signaling is regulated by the formation of an asymmetric kinase-domain dimer, stabilized by interactions involving the transmembrane and cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domains, which allosterically activates the receiver kinase. This results in C-terminal tyrosine autophosphorylation and the recruitment of downstream survival and proliferation effectors such as PI3K-AKT and MAPK.

ERBB2 E693K

The E693K substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged lysine in the cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domain, reversing the local charge. This mutation belongs to a class of activating juxtamembrane alterations that structurally stabilize the receptor in an active, signaling-competent conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate_1's and candidate_2's steps for a conformational shift toward the active state were merged. Candidate_1's separate step for increased binding affinity with self was dropped, as the cited source states the mutation class functions by improving the interface 'or' stabilizing an activating conformation, which is adequately and conservatively captured by the conformational shift step. Candidate_1's step for loss of regulatory dependence was dropped because IL-3 withdrawal in Ba/F3 cells is a measure of oncogenic survival signaling rather than a direct measure of HER2 escaping dependence on a cognate ligand. All candidates identified the increase in catalytic activity, which was retained.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
