Wild type

ERBB2 (HER2) is an orphan receptor tyrosine kinase that has no known direct ligand. It normally functions by heterodimerizing with ligand-bound members of the EGFR family (such as EGFR or HER3). This dimerization relieves the autoinhibitory effect of its intracellular juxtamembrane segment, activating the kinase domain and leading to autophosphorylation of its C-terminal tail at residues such as Y1248, which subsequently engages downstream signaling pathways.

ERBB2 E717K

The E717K substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged lysine at the C-terminal end of the intracellular juxtamembrane segment. This region normally acts to autoinhibit the kinase domain and forms part of the EGFR-HER2 dimerization interface.

Arbiter's reasoning

Merged the steps from candidates 2 and 3 identifying the release of juxtamembrane autoinhibition and the resulting increase in kinase activity. Candidate 3's proposed step for loss of ubiquitination was dropped because the cited source frames it purely as an untested hypothesis ('One possible explanation... may disrupt'), and no experimental measurement of protein stability or ubiquitylation was reported for E717K. Candidate 3's step for increased EGFR binding affinity was also dropped, as the source notes the mutation lies within the dimerization interface but does not directly measure an affinity change for this specific variant. Candidate 2's step for loss of ligand dependence was integrated into the intrinsic activity step, as the elevated ligand-free autophosphorylation is the direct readout of the kinase assay establishing the gain of function.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
