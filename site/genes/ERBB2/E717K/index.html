Wild type

ERBB2 (HER2) is an orphan receptor tyrosine kinase that has no known direct ligand. It normally functions by heterodimerizing with ligand-bound members of the EGFR family (such as EGFR or HER3). This dimerization relieves the autoinhibitory effect of its intracellular juxtamembrane segment, activating the kinase domain and leading to autophosphorylation of its C-terminal tail at residues such as Y1248, which subsequently engages downstream signaling pathways.

ERBB2 E717K

The E717K substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged lysine at the C-terminal end of the intracellular juxtamembrane segment. This region normally acts to autoinhibit the kinase domain and forms part of the EGFR-HER2 dimerization interface.
