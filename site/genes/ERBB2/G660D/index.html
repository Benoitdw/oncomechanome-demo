Wild type

HER2 (ERBB2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that lacks a known ligand and normally remains in an untethered ectodomain conformation. It initiates signaling by heterodimerizing with a ligand-bound ERBB family partner, such as EGFR or HER3. This interaction aligns their transmembrane helices and permits their intracellular kinase domains to form an asymmetric dimer, in which the partner allosterically activates the HER2 kinase domain to drive downstream signaling.

ERBB2 G660D

The G660D substitution replaces a small glycine with a bulky, polar aspartate within the S656-xxx-G660 dimerization motif of the transmembrane domain. This introduces a polar side chain into the membrane environment, which disrupts the native helix-helix packing and shifts the conformational equilibrium of the transmembrane segment toward an arrangement that favors receptor homodimerization.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
