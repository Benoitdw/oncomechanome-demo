Wild type

ERBB2 (HER2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase of the ErbB family that lacks a known direct ligand but forms active heterodimers with ligand-bound EGFR, ERBB3, or ERBB4. In its inactive state, the kinase domain is autoinhibited with helix αC shifted outward. Residue Leu755 is located in the N-lobe on the β3 strand next to the αC helix, where its side chain packs against helix αC and its backbone amide hydrogen-bonds with Val790 on the β5 strand to stabilize the local β-sheet. Type II inhibitors like lapatinib specifically bind the inactive kinase conformation.

ERBB2 L755P

The substitution of proline for leucine at position 755 replaces the hydrophobic side chain and removes the backbone amide hydrogen. This breaks the β3–β5 backbone hydrogen bond to Val790, locally destabilizing the β-sheet, altering the packing against helix αC, and triggering structural rearrangements of the hinge region and the glycine-rich P-loop.

Arbiter's reasoning

The three candidates broadly agreed on the molecular mechanism, although candidate 1 omitted the structural collapse of the drug-binding pocket and neratinib resistance. Candidate 3's structure was selected as the most complete, separating the structural consequences into a shift toward the active state and a shift toward a closed pocket, both stemming from local β-sheet destabilization. Steps describing decreased affinity for afatinib and AEE788 from candidate 2 were dropped, as lapatinib and neratinib sufficiently illustrate the divergent resistance mechanisms with strong evidence. Candidate 3's edge structure was preserved but augmented with the dual causality for lapatinib resistance, which arises from both the active-state shift and the contracted pocket.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
