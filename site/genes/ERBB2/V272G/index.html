Wild type

ERBB2 (HER2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that does not bind a cognate ligand. Instead, it normally functions by heterodimerizing with ligand-bound members of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family, such as ERBB3 or ERBB4. Dimerization activates its intracellular kinase domain, which trans-phosphorylates the receptor's C-terminal tail and stimulates downstream signaling cascades, including the MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT pathways, to regulate cell proliferation and survival. The extracellular domain contains a cysteine-rich subdomain II that mediates receptor dimerization and is ordinarily held in a conformation that restrains basal signaling.

ERBB2 V272G

The substitution replaces valine 272 with glycine in the extracellular cysteine-rich subdomain II, a region involved in receptor dimerization. This removes a hydrophobic side chain and introduces local backbone flexibility, leading to constitutive activation without a resolved structural mechanism.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
