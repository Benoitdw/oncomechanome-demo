Wild type

ERBB3/HER3 is a catalytically impaired pseudokinase belonging to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) family. It possesses negligible intrinsic phosphotransferase activity and instead functions as a powerful signal amplifier by serving as an allosteric activator in asymmetric kinase-domain heterodimers with kinase-competent partners, most notably ERBB2/HER2 and EGFR. In this complex, ERBB3 occupies the activator position to switch on the partner's receiver kinase, which then trans-phosphorylates the C-terminal tail of ERBB3 to recruit downstream effectors like PI3K. This activation is stringently gated: in the absence of ligand, ERBB3 is sequestered in inactive homodimers; upon binding a neuregulin ligand to its extracellular domain, ERBB3 releases these self-associations to heterodimerize with its kinase-active partners.

ERBB3 E928G

The E928G substitution (often numbered E909G in structural literature) replaces a negatively charged glutamate residue directly at the activator/receiver interface of the asymmetric kinase dimer. In the wild-type protein, this side chain creates an unfavorable electrostatic network by generating repulsion against opposing residues on the receiver kinase. Substituting a glycine removes this charge and reduces steric bulk, which allows the interface to pack more closely and firmly toward the partner's alpha-C helix without altering the overall fold of the kinase domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

The steps from all three candidates were merged to construct a comprehensive pathway. The core mechanism involves a concurrent decrease in self-association (ERBB3 homodimers) and an increase in heterodimerization affinity for both ERBB2 and EGFR, coupled with an enhanced intrinsic capacity to allosterically activate those receiver kinases. These concurrent structural changes converge to relieve the receptor's dependence on neuregulin, allowing ligand-independent signaling. Candidate 2 and 3's well-supported observation of a gained interaction with FGFR1 was included, along with Candidate 3's consequence of a novel MEK/ERK pathway coupling. The 'Interactome rewiring' summary steps proposed by Candidates 1 and 2 were dropped because the individual interaction gains and losses already explicitly capture the altered partner repertoire.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
