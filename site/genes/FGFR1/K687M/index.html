Wild type

FGFR1 is a cell-surface receptor tyrosine kinase. Binding of its cognate ligands, fibroblast growth factors (FGFs), promotes receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation at specific tyrosine residues within the intracellular kinase domain, including Y653 and Y654 in the activation loop. This sequential phosphorylation releases cis-autoinhibition by shifting the activation loop and reorienting the lobes of the kinase domain into an active, closed conformation. The active kinase phosphorylates substrates such as FRS2 and PLCG1, propagating signals down the RAS/MAPK and PI3K/AKT pathways.

FGFR1 K687M

The K687M substitution (frequently denoted K656M in the literature, using an alternative isoform numbering) replaces a highly conserved, positively charged lysine in the kinase activation loop with a neutral, flexible methionine. Because this lysine normally contributes to stabilizing the autoinhibitory, unphosphorylated state of the activation loop, its mutation mimics the conformational effects of ligand-induced phosphorylation, relieving cis-autoinhibitory restraints and reorienting the activation loop.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidates 1 and 2 correctly identified that K687M corresponds to the well-documented K656M activation loop mutation (using the alternative isoform numbering). Their proposed 'Conformational equilibrium shift (toward: active state)' and 'Increase in intrinsic activity (of: catalytic)' steps were merged. Candidate 2's quote from PMID:34272467 best supports the conformational shift, while PMC6352210 directly confirms that the K656M substitution largely increases receptor kinase activity. Candidate 2's 'Loss of regulatory dependence' step was dropped because it relied solely on homology to FGFR3 K650M; as candidate 1 noted, activating mutations at this FGFR1 position may not fully abolish ligand dependence, and no direct evidence of ligand-independent signaling specifically for FGFR1 K687M/K656M was retrieved. Candidate 3's intrinsic activity step was merged into the final graph.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
