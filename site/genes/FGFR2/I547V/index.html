Wild type

Fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase whose intracellular kinase domain is held in an autoinhibited state by a network of intramolecular restraints, including a hydrophobic 'DFG latch' that pins the activation loop. Activation typically requires extracellular binding of FGF ligands, which induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of the activation-loop tyrosines, releasing these restraints and fully activating the kinase to engage downstream signaling effectors.

FGFR2 I547V

The I547V substitution replaces an isoleucine residue in the αC–β4 loop of the kinase domain with a smaller valine. This reduces the hydrophobic bulk within the DFG latch cluster, weakening the intramolecular contacts that normally hold the kinase in its autoinhibited conformation without directly altering the ATP-binding site.
