Wild type

Fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) is a receptor tyrosine kinase whose intracellular kinase domain is held in an autoinhibited state by a network of intramolecular restraints, including a hydrophobic 'DFG latch' that pins the activation loop. Activation typically requires extracellular binding of FGF ligands, which induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of the activation-loop tyrosines, releasing these restraints and fully activating the kinase to engage downstream signaling effectors.

FGFR2 I547V

The I547V substitution replaces an isoleucine residue in the αC–β4 loop of the kinase domain with a smaller valine. This reduces the hydrophobic bulk within the DFG latch cluster, weakening the intramolecular contacts that normally hold the kinase in its autoinhibited conformation without directly altering the ATP-binding site.

Arbiter's reasoning

The steps were synthesized from all three candidates. The structural consequences of the mutation—weakening of the DFG latch (s1) and shifting the conformational equilibrium toward the active state (s2)—were merged from all candidates. The resulting increase in intrinsic catalytic activity (s3) was included without a specific quantity, as the cited source reported a fold-change range for a group of mutants. Decreased binding affinity for dovitinib (s4) and pemigatinib (s5), driven by the conformational shift away from the inactive state these drugs target, was taken from candidate 2. Candidate 3's step regarding enhanced ligand-driven coupling to ADAM17 (s6) was also included. The PD173074 step was omitted to rely entirely on primary quotes that explicitly support dovitinib resistance.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
