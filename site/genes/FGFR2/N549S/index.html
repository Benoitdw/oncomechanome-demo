Wild type

Wild-type FGFR2 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is activated when FGF binding promotes receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation. In the basal, unphosphorylated state, the kinase domain is held in an inactive, autoinhibited conformation by a "molecular brake", a network of hydrogen bonds in the kinase hinge region linking N549, E565, and K641.

FGFR2 N549S

The substituted serine lacks the amide group of asparagine, preventing it from forming the hydrogen bonds that residue 549 normally makes with E565, K641, and the backbone of the αC-β4 loop. This physically disrupts the most critical node of the molecular brake.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the core mechanism: substitution of N549 disrupts the molecular brake, leading to a conformational shift toward the active state and constitutive kinase activation. The steps were consolidated to form a cohesive pathway from the loss of intramolecular contacts (s1) to the conformational shift (s2), which then branches into increased intrinsic catalytic activity (s3) and loss of ligand dependence (s4). Citations and quotes were validated against the provided sources (e.g., PMID: 17803937, PMID: 34272467), correcting an incorrect PMID provided by candidate_2 for the Cell 2007 paper. Candidate 2 and 3 correctly delineated the consequences on ligand dependence and intrinsic activity, which were retained.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
