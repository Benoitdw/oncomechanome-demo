Wild type

Fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) is a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase. Binding of cognate fibroblast growth factors to its extracellular region induces receptor dimerization, which relieves basal autoinhibition and triggers trans-autophosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domains. This autophosphorylation—particularly of tandem tyrosines in the activation loop—stabilizes the active conformation of the enzyme, enabling it to phosphorylate downstream substrates and propagate signaling cascades.

FGFR3 D641G

The D641G substitution replaces an aspartate residue with a glycine within the activation loop of the intracellular kinase domain. This change alters the regulatory segment of the enzyme, shifting the kinase toward its active state and enhancing its catalytic function.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
