Wild type

FGFR3 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated when ligand binding induces receptor dimerization and subsequent trans-autophosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domain. In the unphosphorylated, inactive state, the kinase domain is held in an autoinhibited conformation by structural restraints. These include the 'molecular brake' in the αC-β4 hinge region, and a set of interactions where arginine 669 hydrogen-bonds to and packs tightly against residues in the activation loop, effectively clamping the loop in a closed position.

FGFR3 R669G

The substitution of glycine for arginine at position 669 deletes a large, basic side chain that anchors the activation loop. This eliminates the hydrogen bonds and van der Waals contacts that normally restrain the activation loop, physically removing this autoinhibitory clamp.

Arbiter's reasoning

All candidates recognized the sequence of events driven by the mutation: loss of an intramolecular contact between R669 and the activation loop, leading to a conformational shift and subsequent kinase activation. I integrated candidate_2 and candidate_3's approach of dedicating a distinct step to the downstream release of the 'molecular brake', which propagates allosterically from the activation loop's opening. The final functional readouts were split into autophosphorylation and exogenous substrate phosphorylation to clearly capture the two distinct catalytic targets assayed in the cited literature.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
