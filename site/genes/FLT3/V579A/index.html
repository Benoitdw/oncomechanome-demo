Wild type

FLT3 is a class III receptor tyrosine kinase on hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells. It is maintained in an inactive state by its cytoplasmic juxtamembrane domain (JMD), which packs against the kinase domain to autoinhibit dimerization and catalytic activity. Binding of the FLT3 ligand induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation, which subsequently triggers downstream signaling cascades including the PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathways.

FLT3 V579A

The substitution replaces valine 579, located within the autoinhibitory juxtamembrane segment, with a smaller alanine residue. This alteration removes buried packing interactions and breaks a hydrogen bond, structurally destabilizing the autoinhibitory conformation of the juxtamembrane domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates proposed highly concordant chains for this variant. I retained the folding destabilization, loss of autoinhibition, increased self-association, enhanced autophosphorylation, and ligand-independent activation from all candidates. I kept the conformational shift to the open state proposed by candidate_1 and candidate_2, alongside the supporting molecular dynamics figure. Finally, I retained candidate_3's step for the gain of coupling to the STAT5 pathway, as the sources note that ligand-activated wild-type FLT3 does not efficiently activate STAT5, marking a qualitative shift in downstream signaling.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
