Wild type

FOXA1 is a pioneer transcription factor that binds forkhead motifs in compacted chromatin, displacing nucleosomes to increase local accessibility. This pioneering activity licenses the recruitment of lineage-specific nuclear hormone receptors, such as the estrogen receptor in breast tissue and the androgen receptor in the prostate, to drive their respective transcriptional programs. The protein binds DNA via a central forkhead domain, where the flexible Wing2 loop modulates DNA-binding affinity and chromatin mobility.

FOXA1 F266L

The F266L substitution replaces a bulky aromatic phenylalanine with an aliphatic leucine in the Wing2 loop of the DNA-binding forkhead domain. Structural modelling indicates this causes the loop to adopt a stable α-helical conformation that is more ordered than in wild type, which is computationally predicted to lower the free energy of DNA binding without disrupting base-specific contacts.

Arbiter's reasoning

The final graph merges the candidates into a four-step pathway: the F266L substitution shifts the Wing2 loop into a more ordered conformation, which directly increases DNA binding affinity at estrogen-receptor target loci upon estrogen stimulation. This selectively reconfigures the FOXA1-ER coregulatory network and enhances the estrogen-dependent transcriptional program. Candidate 2's proposal of a gain of response to a non-cognate signal was dropped; estrogen activates the partner ER, not FOXA1 itself, and the increased E2-induced binding is properly captured by the interaction rewiring and binding affinity steps. The unreliable 2-fold quantity for transcriptional activity reported by candidate 2, which belonged to a different truncation mutant in the source text, was excluded. Candidate 3's accurate GSEA quantity for estrogen response enrichment was retained.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
