Wild type

FOXA1 is a pioneer transcription factor that binds forkhead motifs in compacted chromatin, displacing nucleosomes to increase local accessibility. This pioneering activity licenses the recruitment of lineage-specific nuclear hormone receptors, such as the estrogen receptor in breast tissue and the androgen receptor in the prostate, to drive their respective transcriptional programs. The protein binds DNA via a central forkhead domain, where the flexible Wing2 loop modulates DNA-binding affinity and chromatin mobility.

FOXA1 F266L

The F266L substitution replaces a bulky aromatic phenylalanine with an aliphatic leucine in the Wing2 loop of the DNA-binding forkhead domain. Structural modelling indicates this causes the loop to adopt a stable α-helical conformation that is more ordered than in wild type, which is computationally predicted to lower the free energy of DNA binding without disrupting base-specific contacts.
