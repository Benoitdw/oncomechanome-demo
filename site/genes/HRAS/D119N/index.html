Wild type

HRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a molecular switch, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. In wild-type HRAS, the conserved Asp119 residue in the NKXD motif makes critical hydrogen bonds with the N1 and N2 atoms of the guanine ring, ensuring picomolar affinity and strict specificity for guanine nucleotides. Because spontaneous nucleotide release is extremely slow, HRAS relies on guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) such as SOS1 and Cdc25 to stimulate GDP dissociation and allow GTP loading. Once loaded with GTP, HRAS undergoes a conformational change that enables it to engage downstream effectors like c-Raf-1.

HRAS D119N

The D119N substitution replaces the aspartate side chain with an asparagine, removing the carboxylate that hydrogen-bonds to the guanine ring and introducing an amide group that is instead structurally complementary to the xanthine base. This chemically alters the nucleotide-binding pocket, breaking its tight grip on guanine nucleotides while creating a high-affinity site for xanthosine nucleotides, without disrupting the protein's catalytic machinery or effector-binding interfaces.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
