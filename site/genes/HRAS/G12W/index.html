Wild type

HRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a binary switch in signal transduction, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. The transition to the active state is catalyzed by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) in response to upstream receptor signals. Once active, HRAS engages downstream effectors such as the RAF/MAPK and PI3K pathways. Signal termination is dependent on GTP hydrolysis to GDP; because the intrinsic GTPase activity of HRAS is very slow, timely inactivation strictly requires GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) like p120GAP and neurofibromin (NF1), which provide a catalytic arginine finger to the active site to accelerate hydrolysis.

HRAS G12W

The G12W mutation replaces the side-chain-free glycine at position 12 in the phosphate-binding P-loop with a bulky tryptophan. This large indole side chain sterically obstructs the active site, interfering both with the intrinsic coordination of the catalytic water and glutamine 61, and with the insertion of the arginine finger from GAPs.
