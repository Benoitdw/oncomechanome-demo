Wild type

HRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a binary switch in signal transduction, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. The transition to the active state is catalyzed by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) in response to upstream receptor signals. Once active, HRAS engages downstream effectors such as the RAF/MAPK and PI3K pathways. Signal termination is dependent on GTP hydrolysis to GDP; because the intrinsic GTPase activity of HRAS is very slow, timely inactivation strictly requires GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) like p120GAP and neurofibromin (NF1), which provide a catalytic arginine finger to the active site to accelerate hydrolysis.

HRAS G12W

The G12W mutation replaces the side-chain-free glycine at position 12 in the phosphate-binding P-loop with a bulky tryptophan. This large indole side chain sterically obstructs the active site, interfering both with the intrinsic coordination of the catalytic water and glutamine 61, and with the insertion of the arginine finger from GAPs.

Arbiter's reasoning

All three candidates correctly identified that the mutation impairs both intrinsic and GAP-stimulated GTP hydrolysis, leading to the accumulation of active, GTP-bound HRAS and constitutive downstream signaling. Candidate 3's use of 'Loss of regulatory dependence' was selected to describe the resulting uncoupling from upstream inputs, supported by a quote from a retrieved paper. For the failure of GAP stimulation, Candidate 2's label 'Loss of response to a cognate signal' was chosen over Candidate 3's 'Loss of an activity', as GAP is an upstream regulatory partner that normally activates the GTPase function. Candidate 3's exact quantity for the decrease in intrinsic hydrolysis (from PMC3354961) was verified via an external search and retained. The final graph merges these well-sourced steps.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
