Wild type

IFN-γR2 is the signal-transducing chain of the heterodimeric interferon-γ receptor. It forms a complex with IFN-γR1 upon IFN-γ binding, bringing together JAK1 and JAK2 kinases to activate STAT1 and drive downstream gamma-activated sequence (GAS) transcription. The wild-type extracellular domain folds in the endoplasmic reticulum with proper N-glycan processing and matures to the cell surface.

IFNGR2 S124F

The S124F substitution replaces a conserved polar serine with a bulky hydrophobic phenylalanine in the extracellular domain, destabilizing its fold without creating a new N-glycosylation site.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates' models were merged. Step 1 describes the folding destabilization of the extracellular domain (supported by all candidates). Step 2 describes the resulting impaired maturation and reduced surface abundance (supported by candidates 2 and 3). Step 3 captures the decreased intrinsic signalling capability of the receptors that do reach the surface (supported by all candidates). Candidate 3's separate step regarding a potential direct structural requirement for Ser124 was incorporated into the note for Step 3, as it contributes to the same functional defect. The quantity from candidate 2 was retained.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
