Wild type

IFNGR2 encodes the non-ligand-binding subunit of the heterodimeric interferon-gamma receptor. Upon binding of an IFN-γ homodimer to two IFNGR1 subunits, two IFNGR2 subunits dock onto the resulting 2:2 IFN-γ-IFNGR1 intermediate, forming a complete hexameric signalling complex. This docking occurs at the plasma membrane and is facilitated by the partitioning of IFNGR2 into sphingolipid and cholesterol nanodomains. The complete assembly juxtaposes the receptor-associated JAK1 and JAK2 kinases, enabling trans-phosphorylation and subsequent activation of STAT1. The extracellular domain of wild-type IFNGR2 is normally N-glycosylated at distinct sites and lacks a consensus motif at threonine 168.

IFNGR2 T168N

The T168N substitution, together with the adjacent sequence, creates a new consensus N-glycosylation site (Asn-Ser-Thr) in the extracellular domain of IFNGR2. The mutant protein is correctly folded and expressed at the cell surface but carries an additional, bulky N-acetyllactosamine-type glycan at residue 168, which lies directly at the interface where IFNGR2 contacts the IFNGR1 stem.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
