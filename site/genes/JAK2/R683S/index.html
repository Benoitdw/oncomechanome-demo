Wild type

Janus kinase 2 (JAK2) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase that associates with the cytoplasmic tails of cytokine receptors. In the unstimulated basal state, it is held in an inactive conformation by its pseudokinase domain (JH2), which packs against the back of the tyrosine kinase domain (JH1) to prevent trans-phosphorylation. This cis autoinhibitory interface includes a salt bridge between Arg683 in the pseudokinase domain and Asp873 in the kinase domain. Upon cytokine binding, receptor rearrangement relieves this restraint, permitting JAK2 dimerization, trans-phosphorylation of the JH1 activation loop, and subsequent activation of downstream STAT signaling.

JAK2 R683S

The substitution of serine for arginine at position 683 removes the positively charged side chain that normally forms the interdomain salt bridge with Asp873. This destabilizes the autoinhibitory interface between the pseudokinase and kinase domains without causing misfolding or local structural changes within the pseudokinase domain itself.

Arbiter's reasoning

I merged the steps from all three candidates into a single mechanism chain. I adopted candidate 3's well-supported observation that the higher dimeric fraction is a consequence of the shifted conformational equilibrium rather than an inherently strengthened self-association interface; therefore, I omitted candidate 1's 'Increase binding affinity' step for JAK2 dimerization. I retained candidate 1's addition of a 'Gain of regulatory dependence' step, as the literature explicitly reports that the hyperactivation of R683S depends on ATP binding in the pseudokinase domain, an input not required for normal wild-type signaling. I retained candidate 3's quantity for the fraction of dimeric receptor, verifying its source through an additional search.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
