Wild type

KCNK17 encodes the two-pore domain potassium channel TASK-4 (or TALK-2). Two TASK-4 subunits assemble to form a voltage-independent background channel that conducts outward potassium currents to stabilize the resting membrane potential. The channel is gated by extracellular pH, with its open probability increasing upon alkalization. In the human heart, TASK-4 is strongly expressed in the conduction system, particularly in Purkinje fibers.

KCNK17 G88R

The G88R substitution replaces a highly conserved, small glycine with a bulky, positively charged arginine in the extracellular loop between the first transmembrane segment and the first pore domain, immediately above the selectivity filter. The large side chain relieves a gating restraint normally imposed by the small residue, physically altering the channel's permeation pathway and gating behavior without changing its plasma membrane abundance or pH-dependent activation.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
