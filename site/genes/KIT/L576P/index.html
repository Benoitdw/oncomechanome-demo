Wild type

KIT is a type III receptor tyrosine kinase activated by stem cell factor (SCF). Ligand binding to the extracellular domain induces receptor dimerization, which relieves an autoinhibitory constraint imposed by the cytosolic juxtamembrane segment on the kinase domain. This release permits trans-autophosphorylation and downstream signaling.

KIT L576P

The substitution replaces a leucine in the juxtamembrane domain with a rigid proline, disrupting the helical structure of the segment and preventing it from properly packing against the kinase domain to maintain autoinhibition.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates proposed consistent mechanisms and were merged. The 'loss of autoinhibition' step from candidates 1 and 2 was kept as the root event, triggering a shift toward the active kinase conformation (all candidates). This leads to ligand-independent activation and constitutive catalytic activity, which were recorded as sequential downstream steps based on candidates 1 and 2. The active-state bias also causes a reduction in binding affinity for imatinib, which selectively binds the inactive state; this was included as a separate consequence of the conformational shift alongside the quantitative modeling data from candidates 1 and 2. No steps were dropped.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
