Wild type

KIT encodes a type III receptor tyrosine kinase that resides at the cell membrane and is activated by its cognate ligand, stem cell factor (SCF). In the unliganded state, the cytosolic juxtamembrane domain folds against the N-terminal kinase lobe, maintaining the kinase in an autoinhibited conformation. SCF binding drives receptor dimerization and relieves this autoinhibition, permitting trans-autophosphorylation of the kinase domain and the subsequent activation of downstream signaling pathways, such as PI3K/AKT and MAPK. The inactive conformation of the ATP-binding pocket is the target of type II tyrosine kinase inhibitors like imatinib.

KIT N655K

The N655K missense variant substitutes a positively charged lysine for a neutral asparagine in the TK1 lobe of the kinase domain, within the region encoding the ATP-binding pocket. The K655 side chain forms a novel hydrogen bond with the neighboring N649 residue, which locally reshapes the pocket by pulling the V654-containing loop away from the inhibitor-binding site. This conformational shift impairs the hydrophobic contacts required for the binding of type I inhibitors such as midostaurin, and the altered kinase domain permits constitutive activation of the receptor.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 2's steps for ligand-independent activation, constitutive autophosphorylation, conformational shift of the V654 loop, and decreased midostaurin affinity were kept, supported by molecular dynamics and biochemical data in the D816V/N655K background. Candidate 1's step for loss of autoinhibition was dropped because its evidence describes exon 13 variants as a class rather than N655K specifically. Candidate 1's steps for decreased binding affinity to several other tyrosine kinase inhibitors (imatinib, sunitinib, regorafenib, cabozantinib, nilotinib) were also dropped, as the cited sources report cellular viability and clinical resistance rather than direct biochemical binding or target engagement. Candidate 3's graph was partially integrated into the ligand independence step.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
