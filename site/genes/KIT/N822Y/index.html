Wild type

KIT is a receptor tyrosine kinase that coordinates cell proliferation and survival. In the absence of its cognate ligand, stem cell factor (SCF), the receptor is held in an inactive state by its juxtamembrane region and by the activation loop of the kinase domain, which blocks the catalytic cleft. The binding of SCF induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation, shifting the kinase into an active conformation to drive downstream MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and JAK/STAT signaling pathways. Type II inhibitors like imatinib and sunitinib bind specifically to the inactive conformation of the kinase domain.

KIT N822Y

The N822Y substitution replaces an asparagine residue in the activation loop of the kinase domain with a bulkier, aromatic tyrosine. This alteration in the local structural motif destabilizes the inactive conformation of the activation loop, interfering with its auto-inhibitory function.

Arbiter's reasoning

I merged the candidates' shared observations on the conformational shift, constitutive activity, ligand-independent signaling, and inhibitor resistance. Candidate 2's specific steps for folding destabilization and loss of autoinhibition of the pseudosubstrate were dropped because their underlying sources extrapolated these molecular mechanics from adjacent mutations rather than measuring them on the N822Y allele directly. Candidate 1's quantity for sunitinib was omitted because the quoted GR50 is a measure of cellular growth inhibition rather than a direct binding affinity. Steps were ordered such that the initial conformational shift causes autonomous catalytic activity, ligand-independent signaling, and cross-resistance to type II inhibitors.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
