Wild type

Wild-type KIT is a receptor tyrosine kinase that controls cell survival and proliferation. In the unliganded state, its intracellular juxtamembrane region (encoded by exon 11) acts as an autoinhibitory segment by inserting into the kinase active site and suppressing catalytic activity. Binding of stem cell factor (SCF) to the extracellular domain induces receptor dimerization, which releases the juxtamembrane constraint and triggers trans-autophosphorylation and downstream signaling.

KIT V559C

The V559C substitution replaces a critical valine residue in the juxtamembrane domain with a cysteine. Because Val559 normally packs into the kinase active-site cleft to stabilize the inactive state, its replacement degrades this autoinhibitory contact.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
