Wild type

KIT is a type III receptor tyrosine kinase that binds stem cell factor (SCF). In the absence of ligand, the receptor is held in an inactive state by its intracellular juxtamembrane domain, which folds into the kinase active site and acts as an autoinhibitory segment. SCF binding promotes receptor dimerization and structural rearrangement, relieving this autoinhibition and allowing for intrinsic tyrosine kinase activation and downstream signaling through the MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and STAT pathways. The kinase inhibitor imatinib selectively targets and binds to the autoinhibited, inactive conformation of KIT.

KIT V559I

The V559I substitution replaces a valine with a bulkier isoleucine residue in the juxtamembrane domain. This change disrupts the packing of the autoinhibitory segment within the kinase cleft, preventing the juxtamembrane domain from effectively clamping the kinase in its inactive state.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
