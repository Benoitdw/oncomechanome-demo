Wild type

KIT is a type III receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally kept in a monomeric, inactive state by its intracellular juxtamembrane (JM) domain. This segment wedges between the kinase lobes, imposing an autoinhibitory constraint in cis. Binding of stem cell factor (SCF/KITLG) to the extracellular domain induces receptor dimerization. This juxtaposition triggers trans-autophosphorylation of key tyrosine residues in the juxtamembrane domain, which releases the autoinhibitory wedge and shifts the kinase into its extended, catalytically active conformation to propagate downstream signalling.

KIT Y578C

The Y578C substitution replaces a bulky, aromatic tyrosine with a smaller thiol-bearing cysteine at the distal end of the juxtamembrane segment. This removes a potentially phosphorylatable residue and disrupts the steric packing of the autoinhibitory wedge against the kinase domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates' steps were merged to capture the sequence of events following the structural disruption of the juxtamembrane domain. Candidate 1's detailed mechanistic steps for class-level JM mutations (loss of autoinhibition, self-association, and conformational shift) were retained and are supported by literature describing JM mutations generally. Candidate 2's proposed loss of the Y578 phosphorylation site was also included, as sources list it among the potentially phosphorylated JM tyrosines. Candidate 3's isolated step of ligand-independent activation was integrated into the broader sequence. The final chain links the structural release of autoinhibition to constitutive dimerization, leading to ligand-independent kinase activity.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
