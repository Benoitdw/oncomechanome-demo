Wild type

KRAS is a membrane-bound small GTPase that functions as a molecular switch in signaling pathways such as MAPK and PI3K. It cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Activation occurs when guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) catalyze the release of GDP, allowing the more abundant GTP to bind. Inactivation is driven by GTP hydrolysis, a reaction that is intrinsically slow but is massively accelerated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which insert a catalytic arginine finger into the active site.

KRAS G12W

The substitution replaces the side-chain-free glycine at position 12 in the P-loop with tryptophan, introducing a massive indole ring directly into the nucleotide-binding pocket. This bulky side chain sterically occludes the active site, blocking the entry of the GAP arginine finger required for accelerated catalysis.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
