Not covered by the vocabulary

The only retrieved structural and biochemical data evaluate M67R exclusively as a second-site substitution in cis with the oncogenic G12D mutation (KRAS G12D/M67R), where it acts as a suppressor. Because all functional measurements—including reduced SOS-mediated nucleotide exchange and impaired RAF1 binding—are compared to the KRAS G12D background, they cannot reliably be extrapolated to the isolated M67R variant on a wild-type background. Although clinical databases classify M67R as a likely gain-of-function hotspot, no biochemical mechanism for the single substitution was retrieved.

This is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of the vocabulary.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidates 1 and 3 built graphs based on a study that characterized M67R exclusively as an intragenic suppressor of G12D (KRAS G12D/M67R). Their proposed steps rely on observations specific to the double mutant, such as SOS-mediated exchange falling below the intrinsic rate of G12D and impaired RAF1 binding relative to G12D. Because these effects cannot be safely assigned to the isolated M67R variant, and the primary source's wild-type KRAS screening data for M67R could not be accessed, no specific mechanism can be established. Candidate 2 correctly identified this limitation; its decision to leave the variant uncovered was adopted, and Candidates 1 and 3 were rejected.

Rejected in whole: candidate_1, candidate_3

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
