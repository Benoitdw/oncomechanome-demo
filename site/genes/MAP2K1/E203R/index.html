Wild type

MEK1 (MAP2K1) is a dual-specificity protein kinase that acts in the RAF-MEK-ERK signaling cascade by phosphorylating ERK1/2. In its basal state, MEK1 activity is restrained by an autoinhibitory N-terminal segment (helix A) that packs against the kinase domain, keeping the enzyme inactive until upstream RAF kinases phosphorylate its activation loop.

MAP2K1 E203R

The E203R substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged arginine in the kinase domain, reversing the local charge at a position that normally stabilizes the autoinhibitory contact between helix A and the rest of the protein.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
