Wild type

MEK1 (MAP2K1) is a dual-specificity kinase within the RAF-MEK-ERK signaling cascade. It is maintained in an inactive basal state by an N-terminal negative regulatory domain, specifically through helix A, which packs against helix C of the kinase domain to stabilize the inactive conformation. MEK1 activation typically requires relief of this autoinhibitory clamp and phosphorylation of serine residues 218 and 222 in the activation loop by upstream RAF kinases, enabling MEK1 to phosphorylate and activate ERK1/2.

MAP2K1 F53I

The F53I substitution replaces the bulky aromatic phenylalanine at position 53, located within the autoinhibitory helix A, with an aliphatic isoleucine. This removes a critical side chain that normally stabilizes the inactive conformation of the kinase domain.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
