Wild type

MEK1 is a dual-specificity kinase that phosphorylates ERK1/2 in the MAPK signaling cascade. In its basal state, the kinase is held in an inactive conformation by its N-terminal negative regulatory region: helix A packs against the kinase domain and stabilizes an inactive arrangement. Catalytic activation normally requires an upstream RAF kinase to phosphorylate Ser218 and Ser222 in the activation loop.

MAP2K1 K57N

The substitution replaces a highly conserved lysine in the helix A negative regulatory region with asparagine. This eliminates the charged side chain that normally hydrogen-bonds with His119 and Phe129 in the kinase domain, disrupting the interface that holds the kinase shut.

Arbiter's reasoning

Merged the steps proposed by the candidates to form a cohesive graph. The disruption of helix A restraint (Loss of autoinhibition) proposed by the candidates was unified into a single step rather than bundled with intramolecular contacts, preventing a multi-value slot error. The resulting structural transition (Conformational equilibrium shift) proposed by candidates 2 and 3 was included. Autophosphorylation of the activation loop was retained as a Gain of a new activity, as proposed by candidate 3. Loss of regulatory dependence on RAF and increased intrinsic activity toward ERK1/2 were included as downstream consequences, supported by all candidates. Candidate 1's quantity for increased activity was dropped because it reflects a cellular pathway output in an overexpression system rather than a direct kinetic measurement.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
