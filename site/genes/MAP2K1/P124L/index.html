Wild type

MAP2K1 (MEK1) is a dual-specificity protein kinase that occupies the central tier of the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK signalling cascade. In its basal state, MEK1 is maintained in an autoinhibited, low-activity conformation by an N-terminal negative regulatory segment (helix A) and an outwardly rotated αC helix. It is activated when upstream RAF kinases phosphorylate serine residues in its activation loop, which enables it to phosphorylate and activate ERK1/2. Clinical allosteric MEK inhibitors bind to a hydrophobic pocket adjacent to the ATP-binding site in the inactive conformation, preventing this activation.

MAP2K1 P124L

The P124L substitution replaces a constrained proline in the loop connecting the αC helix to β-strand 4, directly adjoining the N-terminal negative regulatory helix A. The loss of the proline-induced turn removes a structural constraint at this interface, perturbing the regulatory packing of the kinase without altering the primary allosteric drug-binding pocket.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
