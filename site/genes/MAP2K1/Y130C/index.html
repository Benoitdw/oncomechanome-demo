Wild type

MEK1 is a dual-specificity kinase that phosphorylates ERK1/2 on their activation loops. In its inactive state, MEK1 is tightly autoinhibited by an N-terminal negative regulatory helix (helix A) that packs against the kinase domain and displaces helix C, keeping the catalytic pocket closed. Its full activation requires KSR-scaffolded phosphorylation by RAF at Ser218 and Ser222. MEK1 signaling is negatively regulated by PP2A-mediated dephosphorylation and by caspase-3-dependent proteolytic cleavage at Asp282 during apoptosis.

MAP2K1 Y130C

The substitution of tyrosine 130 with a smaller cysteine removes a bulky aromatic side chain at the interface between the negative regulatory helix A and the kinase domain. This destabilizes the native intramolecular contacts between helix A and the kinase core, relaxing the autoinhibitory restraint and allowing the kinase domain to shift toward a more active-like conformation.
