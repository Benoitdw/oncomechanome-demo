Wild type

MEK1 is a dual-specificity kinase that phosphorylates ERK1/2 on their activation loops. In its inactive state, MEK1 is tightly autoinhibited by an N-terminal negative regulatory helix (helix A) that packs against the kinase domain and displaces helix C, keeping the catalytic pocket closed. Its full activation requires KSR-scaffolded phosphorylation by RAF at Ser218 and Ser222. MEK1 signaling is negatively regulated by PP2A-mediated dephosphorylation and by caspase-3-dependent proteolytic cleavage at Asp282 during apoptosis.

MAP2K1 Y130C

The substitution of tyrosine 130 with a smaller cysteine removes a bulky aromatic side chain at the interface between the negative regulatory helix A and the kinase domain. This destabilizes the native intramolecular contacts between helix A and the kinase core, relaxing the autoinhibitory restraint and allowing the kinase domain to shift toward a more active-like conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates' annotations were merged. Steps for the loss of autoinhibitory helix A packing and the loss of the inactive-state E114-Q214 hydrogen bond were retained from candidates 1 and 2, which result in a conformational shift toward the active state and increased intrinsic catalytic activity. Decreased basal interaction with PP2A and resistance to caspase-3 cleavage (resulting in escape from stress-induced proteolysis) were included as independent effects based on candidates 1 and 2. KSR scaffold independence was included from candidates 1 and 3. Candidate 3's step for decreased protein abundance was dropped, as the cited source does not definitively attribute the lower in vivo abundance to decreased stability. The hydrogen bond frequency quantity was retained from candidate 2, while the ambiguous PP2A binding quantity was omitted.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
