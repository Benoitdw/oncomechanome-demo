Wild type

Wild-type MET is a single-pass transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated by its ligand, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF). In the absence of HGF, the intracellular kinase domain is held in an autoinhibited, inactive conformation. Ligand binding drives receptor dimerization and sequential trans-autophosphorylation of the activation loop, which licenses the catalytic activity of the kinase and enables downstream signaling.

MET H1106D

The H1106D substitution replaces a neutral histidine in the N-lobe of the kinase domain with a negatively charged aspartate. This alteration lies in the ATP-binding region and disrupts the structural packing that normally stabilizes the inactive conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree that H1106D causes ligand-independent constitutive activation of the MET kinase. Candidate 1's step for the loss of autoinhibition was kept, as structural modeling of this class of ATP-binding region mutations supports the disruption of intrasteric restraints. I reordered the downstream steps to follow the causal sequence: the loss of autoinhibition relieves the requirement for HGF binding (loss of regulatory dependence), which consequently raises the basal catalytic rate (increase in intrinsic activity). Evidence quotes from the candidates were merged and formatted.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
