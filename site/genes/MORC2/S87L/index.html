Wild type

MORC2 is an effector of epigenetic silencing by the human silencing hub (HUSH) complex. Its N-terminal GHKL ATPase module regulates this function through a nucleotide-dependent conformational switch: the apo protein is monomeric, but ATP binding closes an 'ATP lid' (residues 82-103) over the active site and drives homodimerization. The closed lid forms a significant part of the dimer interface. Subsequent ATP hydrolysis causes the dimer to dissociate, ensuring that the monomer-dimer cycle turns over at a tuned rate. The ATP-dependent dimerization, coupled with DNA binding, is required to transduce HUSH-dependent silencing of target loci.

MORC2 S87L

The substitution replaces Ser87, a residue in the ATP lid whose sidechain hydroxyl normally hydrogen-bonds to the beta-phosphate of the bound nucleotide. The bulky, hydrophobic leucine removes this direct contact, releasing the lid from the nucleotide so that it adopts a reorganized, partially disordered conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

The shared mechanistic core from candidates 1, 2, and 3 was merged. This core traces how the mutation stabilizes the homodimer interface and slows ATP hydrolysis to hyperactivate HUSH silencing. Candidate 3's initial step capturing the structural destabilization of the ATP lid was kept as the root cause for the reorganized interface. Candidate 1's step for the resulting loss of nucleotide dependence for dimerization was integrated. The independent branch proposed by candidates 1 and 3, documenting a protein synthesis defect that reduces overall abundance, was also included. Quantities for the buried surface area and protein production deficit were sourced from candidates 2 and 1.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
