Wild type

MORC2 is a chromatin-associated GHKL-family ATPase and an effector of the human silencing hub (HUSH) complex, which represses integrated transgenes and transposable elements at H3K9me3-marked loci. The N-terminal ATPase module is monomeric in the apo state but dimerizes upon ATP binding to form a parallel homodimer. ATP hydrolysis subsequently drives dimer dissociation. This ATP-dependent dimerization cycle, together with DNA binding, is required for MORC2 to transduce HUSH-mediated epigenetic silencing. Furthermore, full-length MORC2 undergoes liquid-liquid phase separation to form dynamic nuclear condensates that are necessary for its repressive function.

MORC2 T424R

The T424R substitution replaces a threonine with a larger, positively charged arginine in a transducer-like loop that forms part of the nucleotide-dependent dimer interface. Although the main-chain structure remains essentially identical to wild type, the new arginine side chain reaches across the interface to form an additional salt bridge with glutamate 27 of the opposite protomer. Despite this new contact, the total buried surface area at the dimer interface decreases from 2,778 Å² to 2,527 Å² per protomer.

Arbiter's reasoning

The answers submitted by the candidates were merged. Candidate 2's step capturing the conformational shift in dimerization was integrated into the 'Decrease binding affinity' step for the self-interaction and the 'Increase in intrinsic activity' step for ATP hydrolysis, accurately reflecting the weakened dimeric state under AMPPNP and the three-fold faster ATP turnover. The downstream consequence of these two effects, a reduction in HUSH-dependent reporter silencing, was included as a 'Decrease in intrinsic activity' of transcriptional silencing, supported by all candidates. The increase in nuclear condensate formation was retained as a 'Gain of higher-order assembly', linking it via an edge to the altered N-terminal dimerization cycle as proposed by candidate 1 and 2. All quantities were checked and included where clearly supported by the text.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
