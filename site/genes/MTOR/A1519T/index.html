Wild type

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) is a serine/threonine kinase that serves as the catalytic core of two distinct signaling complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, which regulate cell growth and survival. mTORC1 (containing RAPTOR) phosphorylates substrates such as S6K1 and 4E-BP1, while mTORC2 (containing RICTOR) phosphorylates AKT. The mTOR kinase domain is tightly regulated by an intramolecular clamp formed by the FAT domain, a C-shaped alpha-solenoid, and by the binding of DEPTOR, an intrinsic inhibitory subunit of both complexes that interacts with the FAT domain.

MTOR A1519T

The A1519T variant replaces a small, non-polar alanine with a bulkier, polar threonine in the alpha-8 helix of the FAT domain. Because the wild-type alanine side chain packs tightly into a small cavity in the adjacent alpha-7 helix, the larger threonine cannot be accommodated. This steric clash disrupts the alpha-7/alpha-8 helical bundle, locally destabilizing the FAT-domain structure.

Arbiter's reasoning

The three candidates are highly consistent, extracting the same mechanism from the primary source (Ghosh et al., 2015). I merged their graphs into a streamlined four-step chain: local folding destabilization of the FAT domain, which reduces binding to the intrinsic inhibitor DEPTOR, thereby hyperactivating both mTORC1 and mTORC2. I retained the step for decreased DEPTOR binding (proposed by candidates 1 and 2), as the literature groups A1519T with other FAT-domain mutants that exhibit this effect. The generic 'loss of autoinhibition' steps representing the intramolecular clamp or the DEPTOR regulatory subunit (proposed by candidates 1 and 2) were omitted as redundant; the structural destabilization and the loss of the inhibitor directly and sufficiently explain the hyperactivation. All retained steps are supported by verbatim quotes from the cited evidence.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
