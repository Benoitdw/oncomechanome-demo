Not covered by the vocabulary

The only primary study to directly assay the S2215T variant explicitly reports that it did not lead to increased mTORC1 activity (as measured by S6K1 phosphorylation), distinguishing it from activating substitutions at the same codon. Because the variant behaves comparably to wild type in this assay and no other molecular effects are documented, the schema provides no vocabulary label for a neutral outcome.

This is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of the vocabulary.

Arbiter's reasoning

All three candidates correctly concluded that the variant should remain uncovered. The sole functional study (Grabiner et al., Cancer Discov 2014) explicitly groups S2215T among the MTOR mutations that do not increase S6K1 phosphorylation, in contrast to the activating S2215F and S2215P variants tested alongside it. I maintained the unanimous uncovered status and consolidated the candidates' rationales into the final text.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
