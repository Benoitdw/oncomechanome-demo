Wild type

NOTCH1 is a transmembrane receptor processed by furin into an extracellular subunit (NEC) and a transmembrane subunit (NTM) that associate non-covalently via the heterodimerization (HD) domain. The HD domain and three preceding Lin12/Notch repeats (LNRs) form a negative regulatory region (NRR). The LNRs act as a clamp, wrapping over the HD domain to sterically bury the S2 metalloprotease cleavage site. When a DSL ligand on a neighbouring cell binds the extracellular domain, mechanical pulling unfolds the NRR clamp, exposing the S2 site to ADAM10/17. Cleavage at S2 triggers subsequent cleavage by gamma-secretase at S3, releasing the NOTCH intracellular domain (ICN1) into the nucleus to activate CSL-dependent transcription.

NOTCH1 A1701P

The A1701P substitution (often numbered A1702P in earlier literature) replaces an alanine with a proline in helix alpha3 of the HD domain. This residue sits at the interface where the HD stem contacts the LNR cap. The rigid pyrrolidine ring of proline lacks an amide hydrogen for intra-helical hydrogen bonding, which kinks the helix and disrupts the side-chain packing that anchors the LNR modules to the HD domain.
