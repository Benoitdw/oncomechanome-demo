Wild type

NOTCH1 is a transmembrane receptor processed by furin into an extracellular subunit (NEC) and a transmembrane subunit (NTM) that associate non-covalently via the heterodimerization (HD) domain. The HD domain and three preceding Lin12/Notch repeats (LNRs) form a negative regulatory region (NRR). The LNRs act as a clamp, wrapping over the HD domain to sterically bury the S2 metalloprotease cleavage site. When a DSL ligand on a neighbouring cell binds the extracellular domain, mechanical pulling unfolds the NRR clamp, exposing the S2 site to ADAM10/17. Cleavage at S2 triggers subsequent cleavage by gamma-secretase at S3, releasing the NOTCH intracellular domain (ICN1) into the nucleus to activate CSL-dependent transcription.

NOTCH1 A1701P

The A1701P substitution (often numbered A1702P in earlier literature) replaces an alanine with a proline in helix alpha3 of the HD domain. This residue sits at the interface where the HD stem contacts the LNR cap. The rigid pyrrolidine ring of proline lacks an amide hydrogen for intra-helical hydrogen bonding, which kinks the helix and disrupts the side-chain packing that anchors the LNR modules to the HD domain.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agreed on the core mechanism. Steps from candidates 1 and 2 were used to capture folding destabilization of the LNR-HD interface, and steps from candidates 2 and 3 for the resulting decrease in binding affinity between the NEC and NTM subunits. These structural relaxations cause the loss of autoinhibition by the LNR repeats and shift the equilibrium toward an open state that exposes the S2 cleavage site, a step taken from candidate 3. This culminates in ligand-independent signaling and an increase in transcriptional activity, as proposed by all candidates. A quantity was retained from candidate 3 for the urea dissociation threshold.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
