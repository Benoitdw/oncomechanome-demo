Wild type

The mineralocorticoid receptor is a ligand-activated transcription factor that regulates sodium reabsorption. Its full activation requires binding of an agonist (such as aldosterone) to the ligand-binding domain, which induces an active conformation stabilized by a hydrogen bond between the steroid 21-hydroxyl group and Asn770 on helix 3. This licenses the AF-2 surface to recruit LXXLL coactivators. Cortisol is a partial agonist, while steroids lacking a 21-hydroxyl group (such as progesterone) and drugs like spironolactone and eplerenone bind but act as antagonists. Cortisone has very low affinity and does not activate the receptor.

NR3C2 S810L

The substitution replaces a serine residue on helix 5 with a bulky, hydrophobic leucine. The leucine side chain occupies a pocket normally containing an ordered water molecule, making a novel direct hydrophobic contact with helix 3 and with the C-19 methyl group of steroids bound in the pocket.

Arbiter's reasoning

The three candidates are largely in agreement on the core mechanism. I merged their step topologies, centering the graph on the initial folding stabilization of the helix 3-helix 5 interface (s1) which drives a conformational shift toward the active AF-2 state (s2). This shift is the root of the receptor's weak ligand-independent constitutive activity (s3) and its ability to recruit LXXLL coactivator peptides even when occupied by antagonists (s13). For each individual ligand, I included both the binding affinity increase caused directly by the new hydrophobic contacts in the pocket (s4, s6, s8, s9) and the resulting gain or increase in transcriptional response (s5, s7, s10, s11, s12). I verified the exact quotes for dissociation half-lives of aldosterone and cortisol from the primary literature and incorporated them to quantify the stabilization of the steroid-receptor complexes. 11-dehydrocorticosterone was omitted to reduce redundancy with human cortisone.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
