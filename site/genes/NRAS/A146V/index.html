Wild type

NRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a molecular switch in signal transduction pathways, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Because its intrinsic rate of nucleotide dissociation is extremely slow, activation is strictly regulated by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote the release of GDP to allow the loading of abundant cellular GTP in response to upstream signals. Once loaded with GTP, NRAS engages downstream effectors such as RAF to stimulate MAPK pathway signaling. The signal is subsequently terminated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which dramatically accelerate the intrinsic GTP hydrolysis rate to return NRAS to the inactive state. Alanine 146 is located within the highly conserved G-5 motif of the guanine nucleotide binding domain, where it tightly coordinates the nucleotide by forming a hydrogen bond with the guanine ring.

NRAS A146V

The replacement of the small alanine at position 146 with a bulkier, beta-branched valine disrupts the tightly packed space adjacent to the guanine base. This structural perturbation distorts the local binding geometry, significantly loosening the protein's grip on the guanine nucleotide and facilitating its rapid dissociation.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1's separation of GDP and GTP affinity was merged into a single step for nucleotide binding affinity, as both nucleotides are similarly affected by the disrupted binding pocket and no step-specific difference requires them to be split. The predicted decrease in RAF1 affinity proposed by candidate 1 was dropped, because it relies on a computational model rather than a biochemical measurement and contradicts the activating profile observed in cell-based assays. The remaining steps—loss of nucleotide affinity, GEF-independent rapid exchange, equilibrium shift toward the active GTP-bound state, and subsequent increase in effector signaling—were integrated from all three candidates, using the established functional mechanism of codon 146 variants and direct functional data for NRAS A146V.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
