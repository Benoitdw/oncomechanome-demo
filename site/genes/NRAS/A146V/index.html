Wild type

NRAS is a small GTPase that acts as a molecular switch in signal transduction pathways, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Because its intrinsic rate of nucleotide dissociation is extremely slow, activation is strictly regulated by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote the release of GDP to allow the loading of abundant cellular GTP in response to upstream signals. Once loaded with GTP, NRAS engages downstream effectors such as RAF to stimulate MAPK pathway signaling. The signal is subsequently terminated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which dramatically accelerate the intrinsic GTP hydrolysis rate to return NRAS to the inactive state. Alanine 146 is located within the highly conserved G-5 motif of the guanine nucleotide binding domain, where it tightly coordinates the nucleotide by forming a hydrogen bond with the guanine ring.

NRAS A146V

The replacement of the small alanine at position 146 with a bulkier, beta-branched valine disrupts the tightly packed space adjacent to the guanine base. This structural perturbation distorts the local binding geometry, significantly loosening the protein's grip on the guanine nucleotide and facilitating its rapid dissociation.
