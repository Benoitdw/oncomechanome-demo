Wild type

NRAS is a plasma membrane-anchored small GTPase that functions as a binary switch in signal transduction. In its inactive state, it is bound to GDP. Upon upstream receptor stimulation, guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) catalyze the release of GDP, allowing the passive loading of cellular GTP. The GTP-bound state is active and engages downstream effectors such as the RAF kinases to drive the MAPK and PI3K/AKT pathways. Signaling is terminated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which insert an arginine finger into the RAS active site to greatly accelerate the intrinsically slow hydrolysis of GTP back to GDP.

NRAS G13D

The substitution replaces a small, side-chain-less glycine with a bulky, negatively charged aspartate at position 13 in the phosphate-binding P-loop. The introduced carboxylate alters the local backbone torsion angles, causing the active site to become more open and destabilizing the nucleotide-binding pocket.

Arbiter's reasoning

Steps from all three candidates were merged to form a unified mechanism. The folding destabilization of the P-loop weakens GDP affinity, leading to rapid spontaneous nucleotide dissociation and subsequent GEF-independent GTP loading. The structural perturbation also reduces both intrinsic and GAP-stimulated GTP hydrolysis, and promotes an open switch I conformation that lowers per-molecule affinity for the RAF effector domain. The combination of rapid spontaneous GTP loading and impaired hydrolysis shifts the cellular population toward the active GTP-bound state. Candidate 1's step describing an increase in intrinsic activity for effector engagement was dropped because the per-molecule affinity for RAF actually decreases; the observed hyperactivation is a population-level consequence captured by the shift to the active state. The NF1 binding decrease step was also omitted as the primary evidence cited by the candidates specifically measured KRAS rather than NRAS.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
