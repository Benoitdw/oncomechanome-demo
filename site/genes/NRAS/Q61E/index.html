Wild type

NRAS is a plasma membrane-localized small GTPase that cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. In the active state, it engages downstream effectors such as RAF, and signaling is terminated by intrinsic GTP hydrolysis, which is accelerated by orders of magnitude upon binding to a GTPase-activating protein (GAP). Glutamine 61 in the switch II region is a critical catalytic residue that positions a water molecule for nucleophilic attack on the GTP gamma-phosphate and interfaces with the GAP arginine finger.

NRAS Q61E

The Q61E substitution replaces the neutral glutamine amide with a negatively charged glutamate carboxylate in the switch II region, placing a full negative charge adjacent to the GTP gamma-phosphate and the catalytic water molecule.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1 and Candidate 2 synthesized a mechanism using biochemical data from HRAS and KRAS Q61E orthologs, which share the fully conserved RAS active site, alongside NRAS-specific computational simulations. Candidate 1's resolution of the conflicting intrinsic hydrolysis literature—showing that the variant's high kcat is revealed only under saturating GTP due to its weak nucleotide affinity—was adopted. Candidate 1's computational step for the loss of the GAP arginine finger contact was merged into a functional loss of regulatory dependence step. Candidate 2's step for decreased protein abundance was included. Candidate 2's step on actin stress fiber formation was dropped as it relies on the KRAS-specific polybasic domain. Candidate 3 was rejected for omitting the ortholog and computational evidence that establishes the mechanism.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
