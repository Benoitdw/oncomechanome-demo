Wild type

NRAS is a membrane-localized small GTPase that functions as a molecular switch in signal transduction pathways, including the MAPK and PI3K/AKT cascades. Its activity is tightly controlled by its nucleotide-bound state: it is inactive when bound to GDP and active when bound to GTP. In response to upstream signals, such as growth factor binding to receptor tyrosine kinases, guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) promote the exchange of GDP for GTP, shifting NRAS into its active conformation. This active state allows NRAS to bind and activate downstream effectors. Signaling is subsequently terminated by the protein's intrinsic GTPase activity, which hydrolyzes GTP back to GDP—a slow process that is vastly accelerated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs).

NRAS T58I

The T58I variant replaces a conserved threonine with an isoleucine in the switch II region of the NRAS catalytic domain. Threonine 58 is located immediately N-terminal to the DxxG motif and undergoes significant conformational rearrangement between the inactive and active states. The introduction of the bulkier, hydrophobic isoleucine side chain alters the local structural packing of this regulatory region, interfering with the normal nucleotide cycling dynamics of the GTPase.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
