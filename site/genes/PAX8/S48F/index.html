Wild type

PAX8 is a paired-domain transcription factor that specifies the thyroid follicular cell lineage and drives the expression of thyroid-specific genes, including thyroglobulin (TG) and thyroperoxidase (TPO). It binds specifically to DNA response elements via its N-terminal paired domain and activates transcription by engaging the general coactivator p300 and by cooperating with the thyroid transcription factor TTF-1 (NKX2-1).

PAX8 S48F

The substitution replaces an invariant serine in alpha-helix 2 of the DNA-binding paired domain with a bulky phenylalanine. The mutation does not impair DNA binding—in fact, it slightly increases the affinity for target promoters—nor does it affect nuclear targeting or protein stability. However, the altered paired domain disrupts the direct recruitment of the p300 coactivator, rendering the mutant protein transcriptionally inactive on its own, although it can still be partially stimulated via its retained interaction with TTF-1.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates' steps were merged to form a comprehensive graph. Candidate 2's separation of the p300 binding loss and the subsequent loss of p300 functional response was adopted. The loss of transcriptional activity was kept as two separate steps for the TG and TPO promoters, as required by the schema when multiple partners are affected. Candidate 1's "Decrease" label for the TPO promoter was changed to a "Loss" per the other candidates, as the source reports it failed to induce a significant response. Candidate 2's "Interactome rewiring" step was omitted because it describes the same complex alteration already covered by the loss of p300 and gained dependence on TTF-1. The dominant-negative interference mechanism was included, capturing that the variant competes with wild-type PAX8 for DNA binding sites while failing to activate them.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
