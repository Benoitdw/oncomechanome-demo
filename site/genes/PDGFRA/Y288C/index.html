Wild type

Wild-type PDGFRA is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that binds platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) isoforms via its extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains. Following maturation in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, the complex-glycosylated receptor localizes to the plasma membrane. Upon ligand binding, the receptor undergoes homodimerization or heterodimerization, which induces an active conformation and licenses trans-autophosphorylation of intracellular tyrosine residues. This autophosphorylation recruits and activates downstream signaling cascades, including the PI3K-AKT, RAS-ERK, and STAT pathways, to govern cell proliferation and survival. The surface receptor is subsequently downregulated through ligand-stimulated internalization and degradation.

PDGFRA Y288C

The substitution replaces tyrosine 288, which is buried within the hydrophobic core of the extracellular immunoglobulin-like domain 3, with a cysteine. This removes a key hydrogen bond that stabilizes the domain's core, thereby perturbing the local fold, and introduces an unpaired thiol group capable of forming novel inter- or intramolecular disulfide bonds.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
