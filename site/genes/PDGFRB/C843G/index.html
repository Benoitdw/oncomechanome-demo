Wild type

PDGFRB is a receptor tyrosine kinase that normally dimerizes and activates upon binding PDGF ligands, leading to trans-autophosphorylation and stimulation of STAT5, AKT, and ERK signaling. In Ph-like acute lymphoblastic leukemia, PDGFRB is recurrently fused to partners such as AGGF1 or EBF1; these fusions constitutively oligomerize and activate the kinase domain in the absence of ligand. The aberrant kinase activity of these fusion proteins is highly sensitive to ABL-class tyrosine kinase inhibitors, including imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, and ponatinib.

PDGFRB C843G

The C843G substitution replaces a cysteine with a glycine in a flexible loop lining the inhibitor-binding pocket of the kinase domain. This change increases the hydrophobicity and flexibility of the pocket and abolishes a direct van der Waals contact that the wild-type cysteine makes with ABL-class inhibitors.

Arbiter's reasoning

The wild-type behaviour and substitution effect were written to cover both the physiological role of PDGFRB and the oncogenic fusion context in which C843G was characterized. Candidate 3's separate steps for decreased binding affinity to the four ABL-class inhibitors (dasatinib, imatinib, nilotinib, and ponatinib) were adopted, as they accurately represent the mutant's resistance profile and structurally inferred loss of contact. Candidate 3's root step for a 'Conformational equilibrium shift' was dropped because the increased flexibility and hydrophobicity of the pocket are direct physical consequences of the substitution and are best described in the `substitution_effect` field rather than as a discrete regulatory shift. Candidate 2's use of 'Loss of regulatory dependence' for ATP-competitive inhibitors was avoided, as 'Decrease binding affinity' toward the drug is the more precise vocabulary for an altered binding pocket. Quotes and sources were verified.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
