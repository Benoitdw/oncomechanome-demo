Wild type

PIK3CA encodes p110α, the catalytic subunit of class IA phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K). It phosphorylates phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) at the plasma membrane to generate the PIP3 second messenger, activating the downstream AKT/mTOR pathway. Normally, p110α is held in a low-activity cytosolic state by inhibitory contacts from the p85 regulatory subunit and by the packing of its own C-terminal tail, which sequesters the membrane-binding WIF motif. Physiological activation requires upstream growth-factor signaling: the binding of receptor-tyrosine-kinase-derived phosphopeptides to p85 relieves these constraints and recruits the active heterodimer to the membrane.

PIK3CA A1066V

Valine replaces alanine 1066. Residue 1066 is located in the extreme C-terminal tail of the kinase domain, a segment that packs against the kα8 and kα11 regulatory helices to maintain the inactive conformation. While the structural consequence of this specific substitution is not biochemically resolved, the added bulk occurs in the same regulatory tail that is disrupted by hotspot mutations like H1047R, which perturb the inhibitory packing to expose the membrane-binding WIF motif.

The notes were rewritten to reflect the weak, context-dependent nature of the activation, incorporating candidate 2's point that AKT phosphorylation was reported as similar to wild type and that the variant is five-fold less potent than hotspot mutations. Candidate 3 was dropped as it provided less detail. The reported five-fold difference was not entered as a quantity since it compares the variant to hotspot mutants rather than to wild type.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
