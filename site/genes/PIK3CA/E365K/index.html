Wild type

PIK3CA encodes p110α, the catalytic subunit of the class IA phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3Kα). The p110α protein forms an obligate heterodimer with a p85 regulatory subunit, which stabilizes it and inhibits its basal lipid kinase activity. Upon growth factor stimulation, p85 binds to receptor phosphotyrosines, relieving this autoinhibition. Released p110α then phosphorylates membrane-bound PtdIns(4,5)P2 to PtdIns(3,4,5)P3, triggering the downstream AKT/mTOR signaling pathway.

PIK3CA E365K

The E365K variant substitutes an acidic glutamate with a basic lysine at residue 365 in the C2 domain of p110α, reversing the local surface charge. The C2 domain is involved in binding both the cellular membrane and the p85 regulatory subunit, but the specific biophysical alteration caused by this substitution has not been directly measured.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
