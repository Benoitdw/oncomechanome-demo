Wild type

PIK3CA encodes p110α, the catalytic subunit of the phosphoinositide 3-kinase alpha (PI3Kα) complex. In the resting state, p110α is bound to the regulatory subunit p85α and is held in a low-activity conformation. The N-terminal SH2 (nSH2) domain of p85α forms a tight inhibitory contact with the helical domain of p110α, restraining the catalytic core. Activation normally requires binding of phosphorylated receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) motifs to the p85α SH2 domains, which competes off the nSH2-helical interaction, triggering structural rearrangements that increase lipid binding and enable p110α to phosphorylate PIP2 to PIP3 at the plasma membrane.

PIK3CA E545K

The substitution replaces the negatively charged glutamate at position 545 with a positively charged lysine on the surface of the p110α helical domain. This charge reversal occurs directly at the interface where the p85α nSH2 domain normally binds, severely disrupting the electrostatic interactions and salt bridges that stabilize the inter-subunit contact.

Arbiter's reasoning

The graph merges the core mechanism identified by all candidates: disruption of the nSH2-helical interface (s1) relieves autoinhibition (s2) and triggers a conformational shift mimicking the phosphotyrosine-activated state (s3). This enhances membrane binding (s5) and intrinsic lipid kinase activity (s6), rendering the enzyme insensitive to further RTK stimulation (s7). Candidate 2's weakened iSH2-C2 interface was kept as a structural consequence (s4). Candidate 1's steps detailing gained interactions with IRS1 (s8), EGFR (s9), and Cdc42 (s10) were included to capture interactome rewiring. Candidate 1's proposed change of localization was dropped because a retrieved source (S16) explicitly contradicts it, noting the mutant is not recruited to the membrane more than wild type. Candidate 2's folding destabilization step was excluded for lacking robust primary peer-reviewed evidence in the provided set.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
