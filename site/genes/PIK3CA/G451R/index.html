Wild type

PIK3CA encodes p110α, the catalytic subunit of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase alpha (PI3Kα). It forms a heterodimer with a p85 regulatory subunit, which maintains the complex in an autoinhibited state. This inhibition is mediated in part by contacts between the p85α inter-SH2 (iSH2) domain and the p110α C2 domain. Activation normally requires upstream receptor tyrosine kinase signalling, which provides phosphotyrosine motifs that bind the p85 SH2 domains to relieve autoinhibition, allowing the enzyme to phosphorylate PIP2 to PIP3 at the plasma membrane.

PIK3CA G451R

The G451R substitution replaces a small, uncharged glycine in the C2 domain of p110α with a bulky, positively charged arginine at the interface with the p85α iSH2 domain, disrupting polar contacts and introducing charge repulsion that loosens the inter-subunit packing.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidates 2 and 3 were merged. A 'Decrease binding affinity' step for the p85α iSH2 domain interface is followed by 'Loss of autoinhibition' by the regulatory subunit, which in turn leads to an 'Increase in intrinsic activity' of the lipid kinase. A 'Loss of regulatory dependence' step captures the mutant's ability to drive downstream signalling and proliferation independently of growth factor ligands, as shown in low-serum models. Candidate 2's proposed increase in membrane binding affinity was dropped because the cited source describes it as a speculative consequence of modelling.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
