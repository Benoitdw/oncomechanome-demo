Wild type

PIK3CA encodes p110α, the catalytic subunit of the class IA phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3Kα). It forms an obligate heterodimer with a p85 regulatory subunit (such as p85α or p85β). In the basal state, the nSH2 domain of p85 binds to an acidic patch in the helical domain of p110α, keeping the enzyme's lipid kinase activity autoinhibited. Activation occurs when growth-factor stimulation produces phosphotyrosine motifs on receptors or adaptors like IRS1; these motifs engage the p85 SH2 domains, releasing the nSH2-mediated inhibition and recruiting the PI3Kα complex to the plasma membrane to phosphorylate PIP2 into PIP3.

PIK3CA Q546K

The Q546K substitution replaces a neutral glutamine with a positively charged lysine in the exposed acidic patch of the p110α helical domain. This introduced positive charge electrostatically repels the basic residues of the p85 nSH2 domain that normally dock at this site, physically disrupting the autoinhibitory contact surface.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
