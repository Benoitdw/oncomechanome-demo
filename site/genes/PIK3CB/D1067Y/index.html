Wild type

PIK3CB encodes p110β, the catalytic subunit of class IA phosphoinositide 3-kinase beta (PI3Kβ). It forms an obligate heterodimer with a p85 regulatory subunit, which holds the enzyme in an autoinhibited state through intramolecular contacts. Upon relief of this inhibition by upstream signals such as receptor tyrosine kinases, G-protein coupled receptors, or small GTPases, p110β binds the cell membrane and phosphorylates phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) to generate the second messenger phosphatidylinositol 3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3). PIP3 then recruits downstream effectors, including AKT and PDK1, to promote cell survival, proliferation, and growth.

PIK3CB D1067Y

The D1067Y substitution replaces a negatively charged aspartate with a bulky, uncharged aromatic tyrosine near the extreme C-terminus of the p110β kinase domain. While the specific disrupted structural contact is not resolved in the literature, the mutation alters a critical regulatory segment of the kinase domain and acts as a strongly activating lesion.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
