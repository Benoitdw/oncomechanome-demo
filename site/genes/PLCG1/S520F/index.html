Wild type

Phospholipase C gamma 1 (PLCγ1) catalyzes the hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) to generate the second messengers inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG). In the basal state, the enzyme is maintained in an inactive conformation by autoinhibitory interactions between its regulatory array—including the split pleckstrin homology (spPH) and cSH2 domains—and its catalytic core, preventing access to the membrane-bound substrate. Activation occurs upon recruitment to upstream tyrosine kinases, which phosphorylate PLCγ1 at Tyr783, displacing the autoinhibitory domains and exposing the active site.

PLCG1 S520F

The S520F substitution replaces a small polar serine with a bulky aromatic phenylalanine in the first half of the spPH domain. This residue is located at the autoinhibitory interface where the spPH domain packs against the catalytic TIM barrel, and the mutation sterically perturbs this inter-domain contact, loosening the regulatory clamp over the active site.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
