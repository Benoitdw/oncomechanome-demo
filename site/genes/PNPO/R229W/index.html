Wild type

Pyridoxine 5'-phosphate oxidase (PNPO) is a homodimeric, flavin mononucleotide (FMN)-dependent enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting oxidation of pyridoxine 5'-phosphate (PNP) and pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate (PMP) to pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP), the active vitamin B6 cofactor. FMN is tightly bound in a deep cleft at the dimer interface, where Arg229 forms an essential ion pair with the FMN 5'-phosphate. A nearby active-site loop, carrying Arg225 and His227, coordinates the substrate phosphate and orients its pyridine ring parallel to the FMN isoalloxazine ring, allowing efficient hydride transfer from the substrate to the FMN cofactor.

PNPO R229W

The substitution of the positively charged arginine with a bulky, uncharged tryptophan removes the ion-pairing interaction with the FMN phosphate. The tryptophan side chain is too large for the binding pocket, forcing the adjacent active-site loop containing Arg225 and His227 to move away to relieve the steric clash. This displaces the critical substrate-binding residues without altering the overall homodimeric fold or thermal stability of the enzyme.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates broadly agree on the functional consequences of the R229W substitution: decreased affinity for the FMN cofactor, decreased affinity for the substrate, and a severe reduction in catalytic efficiency. Candidate_3's insertion of a 'Conformational equilibrium shift' step for the displacement of the active-site loop was omitted; crystal structures demonstrate a statically altered conformation due to steric clash rather than a shift in a dynamic conformational equilibrium. This structural detail is instead captured directly in the substitution_effect and the notes of the affected binding steps. The remaining three steps (decreased FMN binding, decreased substrate binding, and decreased intrinsic catalytic activity) were merged from the three candidates, linking both binding defects directly to the resulting loss of catalytic efficiency.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
