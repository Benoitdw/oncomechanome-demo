Wild type

SHP2 (encoded by PTPN11) is a ubiquitously expressed cytoplasmic protein tyrosine phosphatase that acts as a positive amplifier of signaling pathways, including the RAS-MAPK cascade. It consists of two tandem N-terminal SH2 domains (N-SH2 and C-SH2), a central catalytic PTP domain, and a C-terminal tail. In its basal state, SHP2 is autoinhibited: the N-SH2 domain docks into the catalytic cleft of the PTP domain, sterically blocking substrate access and maintaining a closed, inactive conformation. Activation normally requires the binding of phosphotyrosine-containing ligands from activated receptors or docking proteins to the SH2 domains, which pulls the N-SH2 domain away from the PTP domain, exposing the active site and allowing catalysis.

PTPN11 D61N

The D61N substitution replaces a charged aspartate with a neutral asparagine at residue 61, located in the D'E loop of the N-SH2 domain. Aspartate 61 normally acts as a critical anchor in the complex intermolecular hydrogen-bonding network that secures the N-SH2 domain to the PTP active site cleft. Removing this negative charge disrupts the electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonds that hold the domains together, while leaving the non-overlapping phosphotyrosine-binding pocket and the intrinsic catalytic machinery intact.

Arbiter's reasoning

I constructed the final graph by merging consistent mechanisms from all candidates. Candidate 2's 'Decrease binding affinity' for the N-SH2/PTP interface was kept as the root event, distinguishing the physical loosening from the resulting 'Loss of autoinhibition' (proposed by all candidates). This loss of occlusion leads to a 'Conformational equilibrium shift' toward the open state (Candidates 2 and 3). Candidate 3 proposed a 'Gain of PTM site' for Tyr62; because the mutation does not create this residue but rather increases its accessibility, I incorporated this finding into the note for the conformational shift step instead of assigning it as an independent node. Finally, the shift to the open state directly causes both a 'Loss of regulatory dependence' (Candidate 3) due to constitutive opening, and an 'Increase in intrinsic activity' (all candidates) with a documented 12.1-fold increase in immune-complex phosphatase assays.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
