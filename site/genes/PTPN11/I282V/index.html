Wild type

PTPN11 encodes SHP2, a widely expressed cytoplasmic protein tyrosine phosphatase that functions as a key signal transducer downstream of growth factor and cytokine receptors. The protein consists of two tandem SH2 domains (N-SH2 and C-SH2) followed by a catalytic PTP domain. In the basal state, SHP2 adopts a closed, autoinhibited conformation in which the N-SH2 domain binds the active site cleft of the PTP domain, sterically blocking substrate access and keeping basal phosphatase activity low. Activation occurs when phosphotyrosine-containing ligands bind to the SH2 domains, releasing the N-SH2 domain from the PTP domain and exposing the catalytic site.

PTPN11 I282V

The I282V substitution replaces an isoleucine with a valine in the PTP domain, at a hydrophobic residue that participates in the autoinhibitory interface with the N-SH2 domain. This conservative change removes a single methyl group from the buried side chain, mildly weakening the hydrophobic packing against the N-SH2 domain. Unlike other single-base substitutions at this codon, it preserves substrate affinity and does not damage the local catalytic architecture.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the core mechanism: I282V causes a mild loss of autoinhibition that shifts the equilibrium toward the open state, and independently increases the intrinsic catalytic activity of the isolated PTP domain. These two effects combine to raise the basal catalytic activity of the full-length enzyme. The steps supporting this mechanism are represented in a four-step graph. Candidate 1's additional steps for partial loss of ligand dependence and increased cellular binding to GAB1 were not included; the former is redundantly captured by the increased basal activity of the full-length enzyme, and the latter represents a downstream cellular consequence of prolonged signaling rather than a direct biophysical change in interaction affinity. Quotes and quantities are taken from Martinelli et al. (PMC2900904).

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
