Wild type

PTPN11 encodes SHP2, a widely expressed cytoplasmic protein tyrosine phosphatase that functions as a key signal transducer downstream of growth factor and cytokine receptors. The protein consists of two tandem SH2 domains (N-SH2 and C-SH2) followed by a catalytic PTP domain. In the basal state, SHP2 adopts a closed, autoinhibited conformation in which the N-SH2 domain binds the active site cleft of the PTP domain, sterically blocking substrate access and keeping basal phosphatase activity low. Activation occurs when phosphotyrosine-containing ligands bind to the SH2 domains, releasing the N-SH2 domain from the PTP domain and exposing the catalytic site.

PTPN11 I282V

The I282V substitution replaces an isoleucine with a valine in the PTP domain, at a hydrophobic residue that participates in the autoinhibitory interface with the N-SH2 domain. This conservative change removes a single methyl group from the buried side chain, mildly weakening the hydrophobic packing against the N-SH2 domain. Unlike other single-base substitutions at this codon, it preserves substrate affinity and does not damage the local catalytic architecture.
