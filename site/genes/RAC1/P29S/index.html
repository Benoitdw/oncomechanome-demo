Wild type

RAC1 is a Rho-family small GTPase that acts as a binary molecular switch, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. In the active state, RAC1 binds CRIB-domain effectors (like PAK1 and MLK3), scaffolding proteins (such as IQGAP1), and the WAVE regulatory complex (WRC) to drive downstream signaling. Its activation normally requires DBL- or DOCK-family guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) to catalyze GDP-to-GTP exchange, while termination is driven by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) that stimulate GTP hydrolysis. Pro29 sits at the N-terminus of the switch I region, a critical regulatory element for nucleotide and effector engagement, where it imposes a conformational restraint. In the GDP-bound state, a coordinated Mg2+ ion stabilizes nucleotide binding.

RAC1 P29S

The substitution of serine for the conserved proline at position 29 removes the backbone conformational restraint on the switch I loop. The increased flexibility allows switch I to adopt an open, Ras-like conformation with altered residue-nucleotide contacts, while the serine side chain can form new hydrogen bonds.

Arbiter's reasoning

I integrated steps from the candidates, prioritizing the recent biochemical and structural data (Mirzaiebadizi et al., Cancer Gene Ther 2025; Toyama et al., Sci Adv 2019; Chen et al., Nat Commun 2022). The P29S substitution shifts the switch I conformational equilibrium toward an open state, which reduces Mg2+ affinity (Candidate 1) and severely destabilizes both GDP and GTP binding. I included the consequent spontaneous, GEF-independent nucleotide exchange and the impaired GAP-stimulated hydrolysis. While early pulldown assays indicated increased binding to PAK1 and MLK3, the recent data clarify that the intrinsic PAK1 affinity is actually reduced; therefore, I kept Candidate 1's decreased PAK1 affinity and increased IQGAP1 binding steps. I also retained the enhanced binding to the WAVE regulatory complex (WRC) noted by Candidate 3. The resulting active-state accumulation leads to membrane ruffle recruitment (Candidate 3).

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
