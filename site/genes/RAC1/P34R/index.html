Wild type

RAC1 is a Rho-family small GTPase that functions as a molecular switch in pathways controlling actin cytoskeletal dynamics, cell morphology, and proliferation. It cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. This cycle is strictly gated: guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) catalyze GDP release to allow GTP loading, GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) accelerate intrinsic GTP hydrolysis to terminate signaling, and RhoGDIs sequester the inactive GDP-bound form in the cytosol. In the GTP-bound active state, the switch I (residues 25-39) and switch II (residues 57-75) regions adopt conformations that create binding surfaces for downstream effectors, such as p21-activated kinases (PAKs) and the WAVE regulatory complex.

RAC1 P34R

The P34R substitution replaces a conserved, conformationally restrictive proline with a bulky, positively charged arginine in the switch I effector loop. Position 34 coordinates the nucleotide and participates in the conformational transition between inactive and active states. The mutation disrupts the local switch I geometry and acts as an activating mutation, destabilizing the GDP-bound state and shifting the protein into the GTP-bound active conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidates 1, 2, and 3 correctly identify that the RAC1 P34R mutation resides in the regulatory Switch I domain and acts as an activating variant. Candidate 1's step assigning loss of GEF/GAP regulatory dependence was dropped because, as its own note acknowledges, direct biochemical demonstration of GEF independence or altered intrinsic exchange kinetics for RAC1 P34R has not been reported; sources categorize it broadly as a constitutively active or activating mutant. Candidate 3's step for increased scaffolding of F-actin was dropped because actin reorganization is a downstream pathway outcome driven by effector engagement, not a direct scaffolding activity of RAC1 itself. The final graph merges their remaining steps into a two-step chain: the mutation shifts the conformational equilibrium toward the GTP-bound active state, which in turn increases the intrinsic activity of effector engagement, leading to downstream cytoskeletal and proliferative signaling.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
