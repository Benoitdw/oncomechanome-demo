Wild type

RAC2 is a hematopoietic-specific Rho-family small GTPase that cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) promote the release of GDP and loading of GTP. In the active GTP-bound state, the switch I and switch II loops close, allowing RAC2 to engage effectors such as the NADPH oxidase component p67phox for superoxide production, PAKs for actin remodeling, and the E3 ligase TRAF6, which ubiquitinates RAC2 to stimulate mitochondrial reactive oxygen species production. Intrinsic and GAP-stimulated GTP hydrolysis returns RAC2 to the inactive state, which is sequestered in the cytosol by RhoGDI. Aspartate 57 is an invariant residue in the switch II loop that coordinates the catalytic magnesium ion necessary for GTP binding and hydrolysis.

RAC2 D57N

The D57N substitution replaces the invariant aspartate with an uncharged asparagine, destroying the electrostatic contact that coordinates the catalytic magnesium ion. As a result, the switch loops fail to adopt the closed conformation and remain in a relaxed, open state, severely disrupting the nucleotide-binding pocket.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
