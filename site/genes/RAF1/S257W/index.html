Not covered by the vocabulary

The retrieved literature identifies RAF1 S257W as a somatic mutation in lung adenocarcinoma but does not report biochemical or functional assays on this specific substitution. Studies characterizing this hotspot instead assay the related S257L or S259A mutants. Consequently, attributing changes in 14-3-3 binding, autoinhibition, or kinase activity to S257W requires extrapolating data from other alleles rather than citing direct evidence.

This is a useful answer, not a failure: how often it happens measures the coverage of the vocabulary.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 3 correctly observed that the literature documents RAF1 S257W as a tumor alteration but does not functionally or biochemically test it. The primary source identifying S257W (Imielinski et al.) functionally validated related variants at this motif (S257L and S259A) but did not assay S257W itself. Candidates 1 and 2 were rejected because their proposed steps rely on extrapolating data from S257L, S259 mutants, or database classifications, which violates the requirement that every step must describe the specific mutant protein with direct literature evidence. The final annotation is consequently left uncovered.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
