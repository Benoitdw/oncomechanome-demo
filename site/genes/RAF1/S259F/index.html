Wild type

RAF1 (CRAF) is a serine/threonine kinase that activates MEK1/2 in the MAPK pathway. In resting cells, it is held in a closed, autoinhibited state in the cytosol by a 14-3-3 dimer that binds to two phosphorylated sites, Ser259 and Ser621, masking the N-terminal regulatory region and preventing Ras-mediated membrane recruitment and dimerization. Mitogen-stimulated dephosphorylation of Ser259 releases the 14-3-3 clamp, allowing Ras-GTP to recruit RAF1 to the plasma membrane for activation.

RAF1 S259F

The substitution replaces the phosphoacceptor serine at the N-terminal 14-3-3 binding site with a bulky, non-phosphorylatable phenylalanine, deleting the critical inhibitory phosphorylation site while leaving the rest of the regulatory apparatus intact.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
