Wild type

The RET proto-oncogene encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase that normally exists as a monomer. Its activation requires binding to a glial cell-line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family ligand and a GFRα co-receptor, which triggers non-covalent RET dimerization, trans-autophosphorylation, and downstream signaling. Additionally, unliganded wild-type RET acts as a dependence receptor, undergoing caspase-mediated cleavage of its intracellular domain to release a pro-apoptotic fragment.

RET C634W

The substitution of cysteine 634 with tryptophan disrupts a normal intramolecular disulfide bond within the extracellular cysteine-rich domain. This leaves its former partner cysteine free to form an aberrant intermolecular disulfide bond with the corresponding unpaired cysteine of another mutant RET molecule.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
