Wild type

RET is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated by the binding of a GDNF-family ligand complexed with a GFRα co-receptor. This ternary complex brings two RET molecules together into a non-covalent homodimer, enabling trans-autophosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domain and key docking tyrosines, such as Y1062, which recruit adaptors like FRS2 and SHC to drive downstream signaling. In the absence of ligand, monomeric RET acts as a 'dependence receptor' and undergoes caspase-3-mediated cleavage to release a pro-apoptotic intracellular fragment.

RET C634Y

The C634Y substitution replaces a critical cysteine residue in the extracellular juxtamembrane region, destroying an intramolecular disulfide bond. This leaves its normal partner cysteine unpaired and available to form an aberrant intermolecular disulfide bond with another mutant RET molecule.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the primary mechanism: the C634Y substitution disrupts an intramolecular disulfide bond, allowing the unpaired cysteine to form an intermolecular disulfide bond that constitutively dimerizes and activates the receptor. I included the independent of ligand and cofactor steps into two distinct steps for clarity, assigning them 'Loss of regulatory dependence'. 'Increase binding affinity' was chosen for self-association because the wild-type receptor also forms homodimers. FRS2 and SHC binding steps from candidate 3 were retained. Candidate 2's SHP2 step was dropped as it lacked direct evidence in the retrieved source excerpts. The loss of caspase-3 cleavage was recorded as 'Loss of PTM site', adapting candidate 2's step and candidate 3's 'Loss of an activity'.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
