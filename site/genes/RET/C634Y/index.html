Wild type

RET is a single-pass receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated by the binding of a GDNF-family ligand complexed with a GFRα co-receptor. This ternary complex brings two RET molecules together into a non-covalent homodimer, enabling trans-autophosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domain and key docking tyrosines, such as Y1062, which recruit adaptors like FRS2 and SHC to drive downstream signaling. In the absence of ligand, monomeric RET acts as a 'dependence receptor' and undergoes caspase-3-mediated cleavage to release a pro-apoptotic intracellular fragment.

RET C634Y

The C634Y substitution replaces a critical cysteine residue in the extracellular juxtamembrane region, destroying an intramolecular disulfide bond. This leaves its normal partner cysteine unpaired and available to form an aberrant intermolecular disulfide bond with another mutant RET molecule.
