Wild type

Wild-type RET is a single-pass transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that is normally activated when a GDNF-family ligand binds to a GFRα co-receptor, bringing two RET monomers together. This ligand-induced dimerization triggers trans-autophosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domain, which then couples the receptor to downstream signaling cascades including the RAS/MAPK (ERK1/2), PI3K/AKT, and STAT pathways.

RET R886W

The moderately conserved arginine at position 886 in the intracellular tyrosine-kinase domain is replaced by a bulky tryptophan (p.Arg886Trp). This substitution alters the receptor's signaling properties, resulting in a variant with increased kinase signaling output and enhanced transforming potential in cultured cells.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates proposed an increase in RET activity based on increased downstream signaling. Candidate 3 split this into separate 'effector engagement' steps for ERK1/2, STAT1, STAT3, and TCF4, and added an 'Interactome rewiring' step. However, because the cited study measured cellular read-outs of downstream pathway activation rather than direct physical binding to effectors or a shifted interactome, these steps were merged into a single 'Increase in intrinsic activity of: catalytic' step on the RET kinase domain. The note records the specific pathways that are consequently hyperactivated. A quantity for ERK1/2 activation was retained.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
