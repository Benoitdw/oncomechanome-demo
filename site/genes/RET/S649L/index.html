Wild type

Wild-type RET is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is held in an inactive state in the absence of ligand. Binding of GDNF-family ligands to a GFRα co-receptor induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation, initiating downstream signaling cascades. The single transmembrane helix of RET has an intrinsic propensity to self-associate, with the polar residue Ser649 mediating intermolecular hydrogen bonding critical for the receptor's structure and productive dimer formation.

RET S649L

The substitution replaces the polar Ser649 residue with a bulky, apolar leucine within the transmembrane helix. This disrupts the interface for transmembrane self-association, altering how the helices pack together without abolishing the receptor's overall architecture.

Arbiter's reasoning

I kept the three phenomenological steps proposed by the candidates (decreased transmembrane domain self-association, limited constitutive kinase activity, and ligand-independent signalling) but dropped the 'Conformational equilibrium shift' step proposed by candidate 2 and candidate 3. The rotational re-orientation of the transmembrane domains is a speculative hypothesis raised by the authors in their discussion without direct structural or biosensor evidence. Because this unmeasured intermediate was the only causal link between the reduced transmembrane self-association and the constitutive kinase activity, and because the authors explicitly state the activation mechanism remains unknown and potentially independent of homodimerization, I removed all edges from the graph. Conflicting findings regarding the variant's activity level (PMID: 21551259) were incorporated into the notes.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
