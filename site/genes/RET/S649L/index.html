Wild type

Wild-type RET is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is held in an inactive state in the absence of ligand. Binding of GDNF-family ligands to a GFRα co-receptor induces receptor dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation, initiating downstream signaling cascades. The single transmembrane helix of RET has an intrinsic propensity to self-associate, with the polar residue Ser649 mediating intermolecular hydrogen bonding critical for the receptor's structure and productive dimer formation.

RET S649L

The substitution replaces the polar Ser649 residue with a bulky, apolar leucine within the transmembrane helix. This disrupts the interface for transmembrane self-association, altering how the helices pack together without abolishing the receptor's overall architecture.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
