Wild type

RLBP1 encodes the cellular retinaldehyde-binding protein (CRALBP), a retinoid carrier essential for the visual cycle in the retinal pigment epithelium and Müller glia. It binds 11-cis-retinol and 11-cis-retinal, acting as a substrate carrier for retinol oxidation and protecting the chromophore from premature isomerization. Release of the tightly bound 11-cis-retinal is facilitated by the docking of the protein to acidic phospholipid membranes (such as phosphatidylserine). This docking, mediated by a basic surface patch containing residue R234, triggers a conformational change that perturbs the retinoid-binding pocket and lowers its affinity for the ligand, allowing chromophore egress.

RLBP1 R234W

The substitution replaces a surface arginine with a bulky hydrophobic tryptophan. The indole ring cannot be accommodated in the basic cleft and instead buries itself into the domain interface. This disrupts the cationic membrane-docking patch and initiates a cascade of side-chain flips that rotates an isoleucine side chain into the retinoid-binding pocket, shrinking the cavity and locking the egress pore.

Arbiter's reasoning

The three candidates independently and accurately identified the mechanisms reported for this well-studied variant. I consolidated their steps into a unified graph. The core mechanism is a structural cascade driven by W234 burial (Step 1), which both dismantles the membrane-docking basic patch (Step 4) and constricts the retinoid-binding pocket. The constricted pocket binds 11-cis-retinal tighter (Step 2) and accelerates thermal isomerization of 9-cis-retinal (Step 7). Because of the disrupted basic patch and the altered internal packing, the mutant also becomes unresponsive to lipid stimulation (Step 5). These defects converge to severely impair the carrier and release function of the protein (Step 6). The RDH5 substrate carrier impairment was merged into the broad transport defect step rather than kept as a separate node.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
