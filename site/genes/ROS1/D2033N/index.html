Wild type

ROS1 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that normally regulates cell proliferation and survival. In multiple cancers, chromosomal rearrangements produce fusion proteins (e.g., CD74-ROS1) with a constitutively active kinase domain that drives oncogenesis. Within the ATP-binding site of the kinase domain, the wild-type aspartate at the solvent-front position 2033 provides a negatively charged carboxylate. This residue is optimally positioned to form a strong electrostatic interaction with the protonated piperidine moiety of type I ATP-competitive inhibitors like crizotinib, enabling potent kinase inhibition and tumor suppression.

ROS1 D2033N

The D2033N substitution replaces the negatively charged solvent-front aspartate with a neutral asparagine. This eliminates the critical electrostatic interaction with the protonated piperidine ring of crizotinib, replacing it with electrostatic repulsion. Furthermore, the asparagine side chain reorients to form a water-mediated hydrogen bond with the L1951 backbone carbonyl, slightly reducing the flexibility of the P-loop and triggering subtle reorientations of neighboring residues at the exterior surface of the ATP-binding pocket.

Arbiter's reasoning

Steps from candidates 2 and 3 detailing the conformational shift of the ATP-binding site and the resulting decreased affinity for multiple type I inhibitors (crizotinib, ceritinib, brigatinib, lorlatinib, and entrectinib) were merged. Candidate 2's step for increased cabozantinib binding affinity was dropped because the cited source states that cabozantinib binding is unaffected and its potency retained, cautioning against interpreting subtle IC50 changes as an affinity increase. Candidate 3's 'Interactome rewiring' step was excluded because changes to the inhibitor profile are correctly represented by independent ligand binding steps. Candidate 1's single crizotinib step was subsumed into the more comprehensive graph. Evidence and quantities were verified and standardized against the primary sources.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
