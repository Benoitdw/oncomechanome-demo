Wild type

ROS1 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is converted into a potent oncogenic driver by chromosomal rearrangements. These fusions, such as FIG-ROS1, possess constitutive kinase activity that drives cell survival and proliferation. This activity is normally shut off by the targeted ATP-competitive type I inhibitor crizotinib, which binds the kinase domain with high affinity.

ROS1 E1935G

The E1935G mutation replaces a glutamate with a glycine in the juxtamembrane region preceding the kinase domain. This substitution does not abolish the oncogenic fusion's catalytic activity but induces conformational changes in the kinase domain structure.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
