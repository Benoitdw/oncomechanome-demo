Wild type

ROS1 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that is converted into a potent oncogenic driver by chromosomal rearrangements. These fusions, such as FIG-ROS1, possess constitutive kinase activity that drives cell survival and proliferation. This activity is normally shut off by the targeted ATP-competitive type I inhibitor crizotinib, which binds the kinase domain with high affinity.

ROS1 E1935G

The E1935G mutation replaces a glutamate with a glycine in the juxtamembrane region preceding the kinase domain. This substitution does not abolish the oncogenic fusion's catalytic activity but induces conformational changes in the kinase domain structure.

Arbiter's reasoning

The graph includes the decreased binding affinity for crizotinib and foretinib, proposed by candidates 2 and 3. The specific IC50 quantities from a patent source were dropped as they could not be verified in the primary literature text, and the range provided by the primary source was placed in the notes instead. Candidate 2's step for an increase in intrinsic activity was dropped because the source describes the mutant as retaining wild-type transforming activity, not increasing it. Candidate 3's 'Conformational equilibrium shift' step was excluded, as the source states the mutation is 'likely to reflect other conformational changes' without explicitly establishing an equilibrium shift toward a specific state; this structural detail was incorporated into the crizotinib step's note. Candidate 1's single step was expanded to include the effect on foretinib.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
