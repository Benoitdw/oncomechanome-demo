Wild type

RRAS2 (TC21) is a small GTPase of the R-RAS subfamily that cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Activation is catalyzed by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), whereas inactivation is driven by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) that stimulate its intrinsically slow rate of GTP hydrolysis. In its active GTP-bound conformation, RRAS2 engages downstream effectors, most notably PI3Kα, as well as RAF kinases, to propagate signaling cascades that regulate cell proliferation, survival, and cytoskeletal dynamics.

RRAS2 G23V

The substitution replaces a flexible glycine at position 23 within the highly conserved phosphate-binding P-loop with a valine. The introduction of the bulky, branched valine side chain into the nucleotide-binding pocket sterically hinders the optimal alignment of the catalytic machinery required for GTP hydrolysis, thereby impairing GAP-mediated shutoff while preserving nucleotide binding.

Arbiter's reasoning

I adopted the consensus core mechanism proposed by the candidates: the G23V substitution impairs GAP-stimulated GTP hydrolysis, leading to an accumulation of the active GTP-bound state, which in turn enhances downstream signaling. The primary hydrolysis defect was encoded as a 'Decrease in intrinsic activity' (catalytic) rather than 'Loss of regulatory dependence', as the loss of GAP responsiveness stems from an inherent failure of the catalytic hydrolysis step itself. I split the downstream effector engagement step into two distinct pathways (PI3K-AKT and RAF-MEK-ERK) as justified by the sources, and retained the luciferase reporter fold-change for the MAPK pathway from candidate 1. The independent direct effect on PI3Kα binding (a slight decrease in affinity) was kept as a separate, unlinked root, reflecting that the hyperactive signaling is a consequence of increased active-state population rather than tighter effector binding. Steps concerning TCR internalization and actin remodeling were omitted because their supporting quotes could not be robustly verified in the retrieved literature.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
