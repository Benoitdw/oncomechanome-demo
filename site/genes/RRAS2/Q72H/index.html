Wild type

RRAS2 (also known as TC21) is a small GTPase of the RAS superfamily that regulates cell proliferation, survival, and cytoskeletal dynamics. It operates as a molecular switch, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Activation is catalyzed by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote GDP release and GTP binding. In its active GTP-bound conformation, the switch I and switch II regions of RRAS2 engage downstream effectors, including RAF kinases and PI3Kα, to stimulate the MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways. Signaling is terminated by the intrinsic GTPase activity of RRAS2, which is heavily accelerated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) that promote GTP hydrolysis and return the protein to its inactive state.

RRAS2 Q72H

The Q72H substitution replaces the highly conserved catalytic glutamine in the switch II region (analogous to Q61 in classical RAS proteins) with a histidine. This glutamine normally coordinates the water molecule required for GTP hydrolysis. Its replacement impairs both the intrinsic and GAP-stimulated GTPase activity, effectively locking RRAS2 in the active, GTP-bound conformation. While the mutation causes a minor reduction in direct binding affinity for PI3Kα, the overwhelming intracellular accumulation of GTP-loaded RRAS2 leads to a net increase in the recruitment and constitutive activation of RAF1 and downstream ERK and AKT signaling cascades.

Arbiter's reasoning

The answers were merged to capture the distinct consequences of the Q72H substitution. The decrease in intrinsic GTPase activity and loss of GAP dependence (supported by candidate_1 and candidate_3) jointly drive the conformational shift toward the active GTP-bound state (supported by all candidates). From this active state, the steps tracking increased RAF1 binding and downstream activation of the RAF-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT signaling pathways were retained, using verifiable quotes from the provided excerpts rather than candidate_1's fabricated phrasing. The direct decrease in binding affinity to PI3Kα was kept as an independent branch (supported by all candidates) because it was measured under constant nucleotide conditions. Candidate_3's 'Loss of response to a cognate signal' was dropped as the cited evidence describes a downstream phenotypic resistance to BET inhibitors rather than uncoupling from a cognate physiological receptor.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
