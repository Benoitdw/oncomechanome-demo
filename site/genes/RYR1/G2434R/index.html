Wild type

RYR1 encodes the ryanodine receptor type 1, the primary calcium release channel of the skeletal muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum. The homotetrameric channel opens in response to membrane depolarization (coupled via the dihydropyridine receptor) and activating cytosolic calcium (Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release), releasing stored Ca2+ into the cytoplasm to drive muscle contraction. At rest, the channel is tightly regulated and held predominantly in the closed state; as cytosolic Ca2+ rises during a release event, millimolar Ca2+ concentrations and bound calmodulin act as negative feedback inhibitors to close the pore, preventing excessive Ca2+ depletion from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

RYR1 G2434R

The G2434R substitution (often studied as the isogenic mouse p.G2435R variant) replaces a small, flexible glycine with a bulky, positively charged arginine in the central cytoplasmic domain of the RyR1 scaffold. This region transmits activation signals to the pore, and the mutation disrupts local contacts that normally stabilize the closed state. As a result, the channel's gating equilibrium is shifted: it opens more readily in response to activating inputs and resists closure by endogenous inhibitors, rendering the mutant RyR1 tetramer chronically hyperexcitable.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the functional consequences of the G2434R variant, correctly identifying it as a gain-of-function mutation. Candidate 1's graph structure was largely adopted, using a central 'Conformational equilibrium shift' step feeding into the various altered agonist and inhibitor sensitivities, which ultimately drive the basal Ca2+ leak. Candidate 3's precise quotes from Richter et al. (PMID: 9030597) were utilized to provide accurate quantities for the in vitro [3H]ryanodine binding assays. The steps detailing hypersensitivity to specific triggers (activating Ca2+, caffeine, 4-chloro-m-cresol, halothane, KCl) were retained as individual 'Increase in intrinsic activity' steps. The weakened responses to inhibitory Ca2+ and calmodulin were modeled as 'Loss of regulatory dependence', consistent with Candidate 1's vocabulary choices.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
