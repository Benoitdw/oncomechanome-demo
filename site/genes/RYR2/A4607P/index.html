Wild type

RyR2 is the homotetrameric sarcoplasmic-reticulum Ca2+ release channel in cardiomyocytes. It opens during systole in response to cytosolic Ca2+ (Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release). During diastole, the channel is kept closed by its relatively low Ca2+ sensitivity. This low-affinity resting state is maintained by a hydrophobic interaction within the caffeine-binding site: a tryptophan in the S2-S3 domain packs against an isoleucine in the C-terminal domain (CTD), pulling the CTD downward and keeping the adjacent Ca2+-binding pocket large and less favorable for Ca2+. Binding of the agonist caffeine rotates the tryptophan, breaks this contact, and sensitizes the channel to Ca2+.

RYR2 A4607P

The A4607P substitution (mouse A4606P) introduces a helix-breaking proline into the S2-S3 alpha helix, immediately adjacent to the caffeine-binding tryptophan. This distorts the local helical structure, perturbing the tryptophan and disrupting its restraining interaction with the C-terminal domain.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
