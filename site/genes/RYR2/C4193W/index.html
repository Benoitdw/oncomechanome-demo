Wild type

RyR2 forms a homotetrameric calcium-release channel in the cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum that governs calcium-induced calcium release during muscle contraction. The channel is gated by systolic cytosolic calcium binding to a pocket at the interface of the central and C-terminal domains. Its calcium sensitivity is negatively regulated by a local intramolecular clamp near the caffeine-binding site: a tight hydrophobic interaction between a tryptophan residue in the S2-S3 linker and an isoleucine in the C-terminal domain holds the C-terminal domain away, maintaining the calcium-binding pocket in a low-affinity geometry at resting diastolic calcium levels. When caffeine binds, it rotates the tryptophan and breaks this contact, allowing the C-terminal domain to move upward and rendering the pocket highly sensitive to calcium.

RYR2 C4193W

The C4193W substitution replaces a small cysteine residue with a bulky, aromatic tryptophan on the helix immediately below the regulatory isoleucine in the C-terminal domain. This large side chain sterically clashes with and disrupts the critical hydrophobic clamp between the S2-S3 tryptophan and the C-terminal domain isoleucine, mimicking the structural effect of caffeine while leaving the caffeine-binding site itself unoccupied and functional.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 1 and Candidate 3's steps were merged to capture both the allosteric disruption and its functional readouts. Step 1 records the loss of the autoinhibitory hydrophobic clamp between the S2-S3 tryptophan and the C-terminal domain isoleucine, as proposed by candidates 1 and 3. Step 2 describes the resulting shift in the conformational equilibrium toward the active state, quantified by the 8-fold increase in calcium sensitivity measured via ryanodine binding, from Candidate 1. Step 3 captures the >60-fold calculated increase in basal channel gating at resting calcium levels, from Candidates 1 and 2. Step 4 records the macroscopic cellular consequence of this excessive gating: an uncontrolled calcium leak from the endoplasmic reticulum, from Candidate 1.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
