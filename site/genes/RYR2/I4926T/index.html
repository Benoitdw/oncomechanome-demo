Wild type

The ryanodine receptor 2 (RyR2) is a homotetrameric channel that releases calcium ions from the cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum into the cytosol, coupling electrical excitation to muscle contraction. In diastole, the channel is maintained in a closed, low-calcium-affinity state to prevent spontaneous leakage. This autoinhibition relies on an intramolecular hydrophobic interaction between a tryptophan residue (W4645) in the S2-S3 linker and an isoleucine (I4926) in the C-terminal domain (CTD). The W4645-I4926 tether pulls the CTD away from the cytosolic domain (CSol), keeping the calcium-binding pocket enlarged and structurally unfavorable for calcium binding. Agonists like caffeine activate the channel by breaking this interdomain contact, which allows the CTD to shift and the calcium-binding pocket to tighten, thereby increasing calcium sensitivity and gating the pore open.

RYR2 I4926T

The I4926T substitution replaces a buried, bulky, non-polar isoleucine with a shorter, polar threonine in the C-terminal domain. This swap eliminates aliphatic interaction surface at the interface with the S2-S3 linker, disrupting the W4645-I4926 hydrophobic contact that normally anchors the CTD and maintains the calcium-binding site in its low-affinity resting conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidate 2's structural steps (s1-s3) were adopted: the literature explicitly links the I4926T substitution to the disruption of the W4645-I4926 intramolecular contact, which normally autoinhibits the channel by enlarging the Ca2+-binding pocket. Disruption of this contact shifts the channel toward a high-affinity state. All three candidates correctly proposed the final functional consequence (s4), an increased intrinsic activity of channel gating, supported by the hypersensitivity of the I4926T variant to caffeine-evoked Ca2+ release in HEK293 cells. The structural sequence was merged with this consensus final step. The slight numbering difference between human RyR2 (W4645/I4926) and the ortholog numbering in PMC6123685 (W4644/I4925) does not affect the proposed mechanism.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
