Wild type

RyR2 is the tetrameric Ca2+-release channel of the cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum. Its pore opens in response to cytosolic Ca2+ entering through voltage-gated channels, a process known as Ca2+-induced Ca2+ release (CICR) that drives muscle contraction. Cytosolic Ca2+ activates the channel at micromolar concentrations by binding to a high-affinity site at the interface between the central and C-terminal domains, but inhibits it at millimolar concentrations via a separate low-affinity divalent-cation site. The channel is also activated by luminal Ca2+ overload (store overload-induced Ca2+ release, SOICR) and by the agonist caffeine.

RYR2 Q3925E

The Q3925E mutation replaces a glutamine at the high-affinity Ca2+-activation site with glutamate. Computational modelling suggests this does not impair Ca2+ binding itself, but instead disrupts the local hydrogen-bond network and rearranges the interface between the central domain and the C-terminal domain, which impairs the allosteric transmission of the Ca2+-binding signal to the channel pore.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
