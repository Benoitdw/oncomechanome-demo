Wild type

RyR2 is a homotetrameric calcium-release channel in the sarcoplasmic reticulum of cardiomyocytes that opens in response to cytosolic calcium. In the resting state, the tryptophan residue W4645 in the S2-S3 linker makes a hydrophobic contact with an isoleucine in the C-terminal domain (CTD). This interaction pulls the CTD down, holding the adjacent calcium-binding pocket in a relatively low-affinity conformation and acting as a built-in brake. The agonist caffeine binds at the interface of these domains, engaging W4645. Caffeine binding rotates the tryptophan side chain, breaking its contact with the CTD isoleucine, allowing the CTD to shift upward and transition the pocket to a high-affinity state for calcium.

RYR2 W4645R

The W4645R substitution replaces the aromatic indole ring of tryptophan with a positively charged arginine. This removes the surface necessary for caffeine coordination, abolishing caffeine binding. Simultaneously, the loss of the hydrophobic indole eliminates the intramolecular contact with the CTD isoleucine. As a result, the CTD is released from its restraining downward position even in the absence of caffeine, locking the unliganded channel into a state that mimics the caffeine-bound conformation.

Arbiter's reasoning

The candidates agree on the core mechanism: the W4645R substitution abolishes caffeine binding and response, while simultaneously breaking a critical intramolecular contact between the S2-S3 linker and the C-terminal domain (CTD). The structural steps were merged to represent the loss of the Trp-Ile contact causing the CTD conformational shift, as proposed by candidate_3. Candidate_1 and candidate_3 correctly capture both the increased calcium activation and reduced calcium inhibition that drive elevated diastolic channel activity (Arest). Candidate_3 uniquely notes the weakened magnesium inhibition from the cited source. The downstream progression from the leaky channel to a partially depleted sarcoplasmic reticulum and reduced spontaneous spark frequency is included, following candidate_1's causality. Step targets and evidence were standardized.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
