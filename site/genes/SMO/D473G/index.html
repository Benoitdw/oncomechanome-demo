Wild type

SMO is a G-protein coupled receptor-like transmembrane protein that acts as the primary signal transducer of the Hedgehog signaling pathway. In the absence of Hedgehog ligand, SMO is kept inactive by the receptor PTCH1. When Hedgehog binds to PTCH1, this repression is relieved, allowing SMO to accumulate in the primary cilium and activate downstream GLI transcription factors. The activity of SMO can be allosterically inhibited by small-molecule drugs such as vismodegib and sonidegib, which bind to a pocket in the transmembrane domain and maintain the receptor in an inactive state.

SMO D473G

The D473G mutation replaces a conserved aspartate residue with a glycine within the transmembrane drug-binding pocket (TM6). This substitution alters the local conformation of key residues lining the pocket and disrupts the structural network required for drug binding, impairing the accommodation of allosteric inhibitors without causing constitutive receptor activation.
