Wild type

SMO is a G-protein coupled receptor-like transmembrane protein that acts as the primary signal transducer of the Hedgehog signaling pathway. In the absence of Hedgehog ligand, SMO is kept inactive by the receptor PTCH1. When Hedgehog binds to PTCH1, this repression is relieved, allowing SMO to accumulate in the primary cilium and activate downstream GLI transcription factors. The activity of SMO can be allosterically inhibited by small-molecule drugs such as vismodegib and sonidegib, which bind to a pocket in the transmembrane domain and maintain the receptor in an inactive state.

SMO D473G

The D473G mutation replaces a conserved aspartate residue with a glycine within the transmembrane drug-binding pocket (TM6). This substitution alters the local conformation of key residues lining the pocket and disrupts the structural network required for drug binding, impairing the accommodation of allosteric inhibitors without causing constitutive receptor activation.

Arbiter's reasoning

I merged the candidates' steps for the decreased binding of vismodegib and the resulting loss of regulatory dependence on the drug. I dropped candidate_1's independent structural steps (folding destabilization of the polar network and TM6) because they were extrapolated from other substitutions or based purely on computational modeling; the computational evidence for the altered TM6 conformation was instead incorporated into the note for decreased vismodegib binding. I included candidate_3's step for the loss of sonidegib inhibition, supported by clinical data demonstrating cross-resistance in patients harboring this variant.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
