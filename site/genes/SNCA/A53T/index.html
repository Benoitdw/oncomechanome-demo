Wild type

α-Synuclein is an abundant presynaptic protein that populates a dynamic equilibrium between a natively unfolded monomeric ensemble in the cytosol, aggregation-resistant α-helical tetramers, and membrane-bound amphipathic helices. In the monomeric state, transient long-range contacts between the acidic C-terminal tail and the N-terminal region mask the hydrophobic non-amyloid-β component (NAC) domain to inhibit aggregation. The protein is primarily degraded through chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA), in which cytosolic hsc70 delivers it to the lysosomal receptor LAMP-2A for translocation and clearance. While it can spontaneously nucleate to form amyloid fibrils, this process is slow. Residue A53 lies in the N-terminal lipid-binding domain and forms the central hydrophobic steric-zipper interface that pairs the two protofilaments in the mature wild-type fibril.

SNCA A53T

The substitution replaces a small hydrophobic alanine with a larger, polar, β-branched threonine in the aggregation-prone pre-NAC region. In the monomer, this reduces the protective long-range contacts between the N- and C-termini. In the mature fibril, the polar hydroxyl group introduces unfavorable hydrophilic contacts into the hydrophobic core of the steric-zipper, destabilizing the native protofilament interface.

Arbiter's reasoning

I merged the candidates' overlapping steps for tetramer destabilization, accelerated fibril nucleation, and CMA blockade via tight LAMP-2A binding. Candidate 1 and 2's step for protofilament interface disruption leading to a distinct fibril polymorph was retained. Candidate 3's addition of the increased RAN interaction was verified and included, along with its provided half-life quantification for the escape from proteolysis. Candidate 1's specific 'gain of new activity' step for the CMA uptake-blocker function was used to capture the interference with LAMP-2A, while Candidate 2's loss of autoinhibition was included as a driver of accelerated nucleation. Quantities were retained where primary sources supported the figures (homologous aggregation half-life, cell half-life, and RAN binding increase).

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
