Wild type

Alpha-synuclein is a presynaptic protein that normally exists in a dynamic equilibrium between intrinsically disordered monomers and metastable, alpha-helical tetramers. The monomer reversibly binds to small, highly curved, negatively charged lipid membranes (such as synaptic vesicles) via an amphipathic N-terminal domain containing imperfect KTKEGV repeats. In its pathological state, the protein aggregates into amyloid fibrils, where the core Greek-key fold is anchored by an intramolecular E46-K80 salt bridge.

SNCA E46K

The E46K substitution replaces the negatively charged glutamate at position 46, located within an N-terminal KTKEGV repeat, with a positively charged lysine. This modifies the repeat sequence to KTKKGV, increases the net positive charge of the membrane-binding domain, and substitutes the acidic side chain required for the wild-type fibril's E46-K80 salt bridge with a basic residue that electrostatically repels K80.

Arbiter's reasoning

Steps for increased membrane binding and loss of curvature selectivity were merged from the candidates to support the shift in cellular localization. The disruption of physiological tetramers and the abrogation of the fibrillar E46-K80 salt bridge were retained as distinct steps driving the accelerated formation of a novel amyloid fibril polymorph. Contradictory reports on fibril stability between N-terminally acetylated and non-acetylated preparations were both kept as separate steps branching from fibril formation, as the literature supports both under different conditions. The enhanced seeding capacity and cross-strain templating of these fibrils were also included. Enhanced liquid-liquid phase separation and subsequent amyloid formation within condensates were preserved as an independent branch. Candidate quantities were retained only where explicitly supported by the provided quotes.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
