Wild type

α-Synuclein is a 140-residue intrinsically disordered presynaptic protein. Its N-terminal domain (residues 1–60) contains KTKEGV repeats that fold into amphipathic α-helices upon contact with anionic phospholipid membranes. This high-affinity interaction targets the protein to synaptic vesicles and helps maintain an aggregation-resistant, physiological α-helical multimeric state (tetramer). When unbound or in an exposed membrane-bound state, the central hydrophobic NAC region (residues 61–95) can drive self-assembly into amyloid fibrils. During fibrillization, the wild-type protein relies heavily on surface-catalysed secondary nucleation, forming a characteristic left-handed 'Lewy fold' polymorph in which the segment around residues 50–57 functions as a tight, homotypic steric-zipper interface between protofilaments.

SNCA G51D

The G51D mutation replaces a small, flexible glycine with a bulky, negatively charged aspartate at position 51. This residue sits both in the amphipathic lipid-binding helix and at the exact edge of the wild-type fibril steric-zipper interface. The introduced charge and bulk disrupt local helical propensity on the non-polar face of the membrane-binding domain, while simultaneously causing steric clashes and electrostatic repulsion with E57 of the opposing subunit at the canonical fibril protofilament interface. NMR and CD studies confirm that the free monomer in aqueous solution remains structurally unperturbed, isolating the physical defect to the protein's assembled states.

Arbiter's reasoning

I built a consensus graph encompassing both the membrane-associated defects and the downstream aggregation changes. Candidate 1 and 2's steps detailing the destabilization of the N-terminal amphipathic helix and the consequent drop in membrane affinity were merged and linked to candidate 1 and 3's step for the conformational shift toward the exposed, aggregation-prone state. The resulting relocalizations (synapse depletion, extracellular secretion, and nuclear enrichment) were integrated from candidates 1 and 2. I kept candidate 2's step on the loss of the physiological tetramer, which also stems from impaired helicity. For fibrillization, candidate 3's disruption of the canonical wild-type steric zipper was used as the physical cause for the loss of secondary nucleation (candidates 1 and 2) and the accumulation of early amorphous aggregates (all candidates). The eventual assembly into a novel right-handed fibril polymorph was included from candidates 1 and 2. Quantities from candidate 1's cited lipid binding assay were preserved.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
