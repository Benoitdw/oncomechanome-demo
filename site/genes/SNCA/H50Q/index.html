Wild type

Alpha-synuclein is an intrinsically disordered presynaptic protein whose N-terminal amphipathic repeats bind anionic lipid membranes and whose central NAC region drives amyloid self-assembly. It exists in a dynamic equilibrium between a soluble cytosolic monomer and a membrane-bound helical state. His50 is the protein's only histidine; it anchors a functional Cu(II) binding site (particularly critical in the physiological, N-terminally acetylated form) and is highly susceptible to covalent adduction by the lipid peroxidation product 4-hydroxy-2-nonenal (HNE). In the assembled amyloid state, His50 contributes its positive charge to the preNAC protofilament interface of the wild-type rod polymorph, forming an electrostatic contact with Glu57 of the opposing protofilament.

SNCA H50Q

The substitution replaces the titratable, positively charged, metal-binding histidine imidazole with an uncharged, polar glutamine amide. This immediately abolishes both the Cu(II) anchoring site and the HNE lipidation site at residue 50. Structurally, the uncharged glutamine can no longer maintain the inter-protofilament contact with Glu57, disrupting the preNAC interface of the wild-type rod fibril and permitting the formation of alternative, highly stable fibril polymorphs stabilized by new intramolecular and intermolecular contacts.

Arbiter's reasoning

Candidates 1, 2, and 3 all capture the core structural mechanism: the loss of the wild-type protofilament interface at residue 50, the formation of new alternative polymorphs, and the thermodynamic stabilization of these mutant fibrils leading to accelerated primary aggregation (lowered critical concentration). I merged these structural and kinetic steps, adopting the precise mapping of polymorph interfaces from candidates 1 and 3. I included candidate 1's well-supported step on the loss of surface-catalyzed secondary nucleation, which is specifically attributed to the absence of the histidine charge. I also included the loss of Cu(II) binding (candidates 1 and 3), ensuring the note specifies this applies to the physiologically N-terminally acetylated form. Finally, I retained candidate 1's documentation of the loss of HNE adduction (recorded as loss of lipidation) and candidate 3's step for increased templated seeding capacity. Amorphous and intracellular aggregate steps were dropped in favor of rigorous biochemical characterizations of the fibrillar states.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
