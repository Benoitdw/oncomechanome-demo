Wild type

SOS1 is a dual guanine nucleotide exchange factor that activates RAS and RAC GTPases. It normally resides in the cytosol in an autoinhibited state, where its N-terminal domains occlude the allosteric RAS-binding site. Upon receptor tyrosine kinase stimulation, SOS1 is recruited to the plasma membrane. Binding of RAS-GTP to the allosteric site induces a rotation of the REM domain and the CDC25 catalytic module, relieving steric hindrance and allowing efficient GDP/GTP exchange on RAS at the active site. A separate Dbl homology domain provides the catalytic activity toward RAC.

SOS1 E846K

The E846K substitution replaces a negatively charged glutamate with a positively charged lysine within the CDC25 catalytic domain. The wild-type glutamate resides away from the RAS-binding interfaces and normally forms electrostatic contacts with Arg1026 and Lys1029. The mutation's charge reversal disrupts this local intramolecular network.

Arbiter's reasoning

The final annotation merges the three core steps proposed by all candidates: a conformational shift of the CDC25 domain, increased catalytic activity toward RAS, and increased catalytic activity toward RAC1. The RAC1 step is kept as an independent root because the sources explicitly state that the structural mechanism connecting the CDC25 substitution to the DH domain remains unknown. Candidate 1's 'Loss of response to a cognate signal' and candidate 3's 'Loss of regulatory dependence' were dropped; the reported sustained signaling after EGF stimulation and basal elevation in tissue do not strictly fulfill either label's requirement for ligand independence or complete loss of response, and are adequately captured in the note for increased RAS-directed catalytic activity.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
