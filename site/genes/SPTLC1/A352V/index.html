Wild type

The SPTLC1 gene encodes a non-catalytic but essential subunit of the endoplasmic reticulum serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) complex. Together with the catalytic subunit SPTLC2 and a small regulatory subunit, the SPT complex catalyzes the pyridoxal-5'-phosphate-dependent condensation of L-serine with palmitoyl-CoA, forming 3-ketodihydrosphingosine in the first and rate-limiting step of de novo sphingolipid synthesis. The wild-type enzyme is highly selective for L-serine.

SPTLC1 A352V

The A352V substitution replaces an alanine with a bulkier, hydrophobic valine in the cytosolic region of SPTLC1. This change alters the enzymatic properties of the SPT complex, leading to a contested reduction in canonical activity and an in vivo shift in substrate preference.

Arbiter's reasoning

Steps for reduced catalytic activity and altered substrate specificity toward L-alanine were merged. Candidate 3's step for altered specificity toward L-glycine was dropped, as the cited source explicitly states that the A352V mutant does not metabolize glycine, contrary to other mutants evaluated in the same study. Candidate 1's graph was not fully followed because it omitted the altered specificity step on the grounds that it was not seen in cell models, despite the source concluding pathogenicity based on patient plasma lipidomics. Conflicting evidence regarding the reduction in canonical activity was included in the note for the catalytic activity step.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
