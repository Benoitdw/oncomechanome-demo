Wild type

SPTLC1 is the non-catalytic subunit of the serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) complex, which resides in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. SPT catalyzes the pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent condensation of L-serine with palmitoyl-CoA, which is the first and rate-limiting step in the de novo biosynthesis of sphingolipids. While the active site forms at the interface between SPTLC1 and the catalytic SPTLC2 (or SPTLC3) subunit, SPTLC1 itself provides essential structural support. The wild-type holoenzyme is highly selective for L-serine and its activity is under negative feedback regulation by ORMDL proteins to maintain sphingolipid homeostasis.

SPTLC1 C133W

The C133W substitution replaces a small cysteine with a bulky tryptophan at the active site interface. This perturbs the local geometry, moderately decreasing the maximal rate of canonical L-serine condensation and weakening the association of the SPT complex with ORMDL regulatory proteins. Crucially, the remodeled pocket enhances the activation of non-cognate amino acids, enabling the enzyme to efficiently condense palmitoyl-CoA with L-alanine and glycine to produce toxic deoxysphingolipids.

Arbiter's reasoning

The three candidates broadly agree on the core mechanisms: the mutant uses L-alanine and L-glycine as alternative substrates (generating neurotoxic deoxysphingolipids), exhibits reduced canonical condensation of L-serine, and exerts a dominant-negative effect on wild-type SPT. Candidate 2 proposed 'Altered binding specificity' for the non-cognate substrates alongside 'Gain of a new activity' for the resulting products; these were consolidated into single 'Gain of a new activity' steps per substrate ('as: new substrate'), as measured affinities for alanine are actually unchanged and the effect is one of catalytic activation. Candidate 3's step describing reduced binding to ORMDL regulatory proteins was retained, supported by the provided literature. The step detailing the dominant-negative poisoning of wild-type SPT complexes was also retained, with an edge from the reduced canonical activity of the mutant heterotrimer.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
