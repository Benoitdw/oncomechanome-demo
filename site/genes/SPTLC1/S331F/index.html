Wild type

SPTLC1 is an essential subunit of the serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) holoenzyme, which localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum and catalyzes the rate-limiting step of de novo sphingolipid biosynthesis: the condensation of L-serine and palmitoyl-CoA. Although the catalytic site resides in SPTLC2, SPTLC1 shapes the substrate binding pocket. The complex is homeostatically regulated by ORMDL proteins (ORMDL1/2/3), which bind to the SPT subunits and, upon sensing elevated ceramide levels, allosterically inhibit SPT activity to prevent excess sphingolipid accumulation.

SPTLC1 S331F

The substitution replaces a small polar serine with a large hydrophobic phenylalanine at position 331, a residue that participates both in shaping the active-site substrate tunnel and in the physical interaction with the N-terminal inhibitory loop of ORMDL regulatory proteins.

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.
