Wild type

STAT3 is a latent cytoplasmic transcription factor that requires upstream signaling for activation. Upon stimulation of cytokine or growth factor receptors, STAT3 is recruited via its SH2 domain to phosphotyrosine docking sites on the receptor complex, where it is phosphorylated at Tyr705 by associated Janus kinases (JAKs). The phosphorylated monomers then assemble into tail-to-tail homodimers through reciprocal intermolecular contacts between the SH2 domain and the partner's pTyr705. These active dimers translocate to the nucleus and bind DNA consensus sequences to drive target gene transcription. The phosphotyrosine-binding pocket of the SH2 domain carries a positive electrostatic potential that stabilizes the bound phosphotyrosine.

STAT3 G618R

The G618R substitution replaces a small, neutral glycine with a bulky, positively charged arginine in the BC loop of the SH2 domain, located on the rim of the phosphotyrosine-binding pocket. The introduction of an arginine side chain increases the local positive electrostatic potential, altering the surface's structural and chemical properties.
