Wild type

p53 is a short-lived, tetrameric transcription factor that responds to cellular stress by binding to sequence-specific DNA response elements. The DNA-binding core domain docks to the DNA minor groove, where Arg273 forms essential hydrogen bonds with the phosphate backbone. Upon binding, wild-type p53 transactivates target genes such as CDKN1A, BAX, and MDM2 to coordinate cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, and its own ubiquitin-dependent turnover.

TP53 R273H

The R273H substitution replaces the positively charged arginine with a shorter histidine residue. This deletion abolishes the critical electrostatic and hydrogen-bond contacts with the DNA phosphate backbone, disrupting the local protein-DNA interface while preserving the overall structural architecture and thermodynamic stability of the core domain.

This is a demo of OncoMechanome

OncoMechanome annotates cancer variants with the chain of molecular mechanisms that links the amino-acid change to the change in protein activity. Each chain is built step by step by a panel of LLM annotators, every step backed by a cited source, then consolidated by an arbiter.

This demo shows a sample of annotated variants. The full version, covering the complete set of gain-of-function variants, will be available soon.
